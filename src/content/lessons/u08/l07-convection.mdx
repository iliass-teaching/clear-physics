<Step kind="starter" title="Where to put it?" minutes={5}>

In a cold country, the heater in a room is near the **floor**. In a hot country, the air conditioner is high on the **wall**. Why do you think they are in different places?

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="How convection works" minutes={12}>

Water and air are bad conductors. But they can still carry energy: they **move**, and take the energy with them. This is **convection**. It happens only in **liquids** and **gases** (fluids).

<Figure svg={convectionBeakerSvg()} caption="A convection current in a beaker of water." width="32rem" />

1. The water near the heat gets hotter. It **expands**.
2. Same mass, bigger volume: its **density** goes **down** (remember $\rho = \dfrac{m}{V}$).
3. The less dense hot water **rises**. Cooler, denser water **sinks** to take its place.
4. At the top, the hot water moves across and cools. It becomes denser and sinks.

This loop is a **convection current**. It carries energy to all of the water.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Convection in everyday life" minutes={10}>

<Figure svg={roomConvectionSvg()} caption="A heater warms the whole room by convection." width="26rem" />

- **A heater near the floor:** the air near it gets warm, rises, moves across the ceiling, cools and sinks. A convection current warms the whole room.
- **An air conditioner high on a wall:** the cold air is dense, so it sinks and the warmer air rises to the conditioner.
- **A pan of water on a cooker:** the metal base conducts energy to the water at the bottom. Then convection carries the energy to all of the water.
- **Sea breezes** (stretch): on a sunny day the land gets hot faster than the sea. Hot air rises over the land, and cooler air from the sea moves in.

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="See a convection current" minutes={15}>

**Equipment:** a large beaker of cold water, one small crystal of potassium manganate(VII) (potassium permanganate), a drinking straw (or tweezers), a Bunsen burner or a small heater.

1. Use the straw to put the crystal at the bottom of the beaker, near one side.
2. Heat gently under the crystal.
3. Watch the purple colour for 5 minutes. Draw what you see.

**Questions:**

1. Where does the purple water go first? Why?
2. Draw arrows on your diagram to show the convection current.
3. Explain the convection current with the words *expands*, *density*, *rises* and *sinks*.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core:** 2.3.2 C1, C2 and 2.3.4 C1(a)(b) (pans and heating a room).

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 22 min, experiment 15 min, check 13 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- "Heat rises." Correct to: *hot fluid rises*.
- "The particles get bigger / lighter when hot." The particles move further apart; the density goes down.
- Thinking convection can happen in solids.

**Link:** this lesson uses density (Unit 2) and thermal expansion (lesson 8.1). Remind students: less dense things float, denser things sink.

**Language support:**

- *fluid* = a liquid or a gas (anything that can flow).
- *current* = a flow in one direction (like a river), not only an electric current.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: give the beaker diagram with arrows; students add the labels.
- Stretch: explain the sea breeze and the land breeze at night.

**Safety:** potassium manganate(VII) stains skin and clothes and is harmful: use one small crystal, do not touch it, and wear eye protection.

</Step>
