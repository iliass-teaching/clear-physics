On a hot, windy day, you get out of a swimming pool. You feel cold, even in the sun. Why?

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="What is evaporation?" minutes={12}>

The particles in a liquid do **not** all move at the same speed. Some are fast, some are slow.

<Figure svg={evaporationSvg()} caption="Evaporation: the fastest particles escape from the surface." width="24rem" />

- Near the surface, the **fastest** particles (with the most kinetic energy) can **escape** from the liquid. They become gas. This is **evaporation**.
- The **slower** particles stay. So the **average kinetic energy** of the particles left in the liquid goes down.
- So the temperature of the liquid goes down: **evaporation cools a liquid**.

Evaporation happens at **any temperature**, not only at the boiling point. This is why puddles dry, even on a cold day.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Boiling and evaporation (Extended)" minutes={10}>

<div class="table-wrap">

| | Evaporation | Boiling |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | any temperature | only at the boiling point |
| Where? | only at the **surface** | **everywhere** in the liquid: bubbles of gas form inside |
| Speed | slow | fast |
| Temperature of the liquid | goes down (the liquid cools) | stays at the boiling point |

</div>

**What makes evaporation faster?**

- **Higher temperature:** more particles have enough energy to escape.
- **Bigger surface area:** more particles are near the surface.
- **Air movement (wind) over the surface:** it takes away the gas particles, so fewer go back into the liquid.

**Cooling an object:** when a liquid evaporates from an object, it takes energy from the object. So the object cools.

- Sweat evaporates from your skin and cools you.
- A wet cloth around a clay water pot keeps the water cool.
- Alcohol gel on your hands feels cold.

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Wet and dry thermometers" minutes={15}>

**Equipment:** two thermometers, cotton wool, water at room temperature, a fan (or a piece of card to wave), a stopwatch.

1. Read both thermometers. They should show the same temperature.
2. Wrap wet cotton wool around the bulb of one thermometer. Leave the other dry.
3. Read both thermometers every minute for 5 minutes.
4. Now use the fan to blow air over both thermometers. Read them again for 3 minutes.

**Questions:**

1. Which thermometer shows a lower temperature? Explain with particles.
2. What does the fan change? Why?
3. Predict: what happens with a liquid that evaporates more quickly, like alcohol?

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** evaporation with particles and evaporative cooling (2.2.3 C4, C5) are Core. Boiling versus evaporation, the three factors and the cooling of an object (S6–S8) are Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 22 min, thermometers 15 min, check 13 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- "Evaporation only happens when the water is hot."
- "The water cools because cold air touches it."
- Mixing *evaporation* and *condensation*.

**Context:** in hot and humid countries, sweat evaporates slowly because the air already contains a lot of water vapour. This is why humid heat feels worse. (Humidity is not in the syllabus.)

**Language support:**

- *escape* = get out. *surface* = the top of the liquid.
- Sentence frame: *"The … particles escape from the surface, so the average kinetic energy of the particles left … , so the temperature … ."*

**Differentiation:**

- Support: act it out: students are particles; the fastest ones leave the "liquid" area; what happens to the average speed of the rest?
- Stretch: explain why a dog pants on a hot day.

**Safety:** keep alcohol away from flames.

</Step>
