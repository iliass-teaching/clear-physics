<Step kind="starter" title="A stuck lid" minutes={5}>

The metal lid of a glass jar is stuck. Your grandmother puts the lid under hot water for a few seconds. Now it opens easily. Why?

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Things get bigger when they get hotter" minutes={12}>

When we heat a solid, a liquid or a gas, it gets a little bigger: it **expands**. When it cools, it gets smaller: it **contracts**. This is **thermal expansion**.

- The particles do **not** get bigger.
- The particles move (or vibrate) more, so they take more space: the distance between them increases.

For the same rise in temperature:

<div class="table-wrap">

| | Expansion |
|---|---|
| **solids** | very small |
| **liquids** | bigger |
| **gases** | much bigger |

</div>

**Extended: why?**

- In a **solid**, strong forces hold the particles close together. They can only vibrate a little more: the expansion is very small.
- In a **liquid**, the forces are weaker. The particles can move further apart: the expansion is bigger.
- In a **gas**, the particles are far apart and the forces between them are very small. The gas expands a lot.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Expansion in everyday life" minutes={13}>

**Problems:** we must leave space for expansion.

<Figure svg={railGapSvg()} caption="Some railway tracks have gaps between the rails. On a hot day, the rails expand into the gaps." width="30rem" />

- **Bridges** have expansion joints, so the road can expand on hot days.
- **Power cables** hang loose between poles: in winter they contract and become tighter.
- Thick **glass** can crack when you pour in boiling water: the inside expands before the outside.

**Uses:**

- A **liquid-in-glass thermometer**: the liquid expands and goes up the thin tube.
- A **bimetallic strip**: two metals fixed together. One metal expands more than the other, so the strip bends. It can switch a heater off (a thermostat) or ring a fire alarm.

<Figure svg={bimetallicSvg()} caption="A bimetallic strip bends when it gets hot." width="30rem" />

- The stuck lid: the metal lid expands more than the glass jar, so it becomes loose.

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="See the expansion" minutes={13}>

**Demonstration 1 — a solid:** a metal ball just passes through a metal ring. Heat the ball with a Bunsen burner. Does it still pass through? Let it cool. Try again.

**Demonstration 2 — a liquid:** a flask full of coloured water, with a thin glass tube in the stopper. Mark the level in the tube. Put the flask in hot water. Watch the level.

**Demonstration 3 — a gas:** put a balloon on the neck of an empty flask. Hold the flask in your hands, or put it in hot water. What happens to the balloon?

**Write** one sentence for each demonstration: *"When the … gets hotter, it … , so … ."* Which one expanded the most?

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** describing expansion and its applications (2.2.1 C1, C2) is Core. The explanation of the order solid < liquid < gas with particles (2.2.1 S3) is Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 25 min, demonstrations 13 min, check 12 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- "The particles expand." Correct this every time: the particles do not change size.
- "Heat rises." Heat is not a substance. Say: *hot air rises* (Unit 8, convection).
- Thinking that solids do not expand at all.

**About railways:** many modern railways weld the rails into very long sections and stretch them when they are laid, so there are fewer gaps. Gaps and expansion joints are still a good example.

**Language support:**

- *expand* (get bigger) ↔ *contract* (get smaller).
- *bimetallic* = *bi* (two) + *metallic* (metal).

**Differentiation:**

- Support: give the three demonstrations as pictures with sentences to complete.
- Stretch: a bimetallic strip is made of brass and iron. Brass expands more. Draw the strip when it is hot, and when it is very cold.

**Safety:** the ball and ring stay hot for a long time after the demonstration. Use tongs and a heatproof mat.

</Step>
