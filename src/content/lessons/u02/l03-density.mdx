<Step kind="learn" title="What is density?" minutes={12}>

**Density** is the **mass per unit volume**: the mass of each cubic centimetre (or each cubic metre) of a material.

$$
\rho = \frac{m}{V}
$$

The symbol $\rho$ is the Greek letter "rho".

<div class="table-wrap">

| Quantity | Symbol | Units |
|---|---|---|
| density | $\rho$ | g/cm³ or kg/m³ |
| mass | $m$ | g or kg |
| volume | $V$ | cm³ or m³ |

</div>

Iron is dense: each cm³ of iron has a mass of 7.9 g. Feathers are not dense: 1 kg of feathers takes up a lot of space.

<WorkedExample
  question="A block of aluminium has a mass of 54 g and a volume of 20 cm³. Calculate its density."
  given={['$m = 54\\,\\text{g}$', '$V = 20\\,\\text{cm}^3$']}
  find="the density $\rho$"
  answer="$\rho = 2.7\,\text{g/cm}^3$"
>

$\rho = \dfrac{m}{V} = \dfrac{54}{20}$

$\rho = 2.7\,\text{g/cm}^3$

</WorkedExample>

**Find the mass or the volume:**

$$
m = \rho \times V \qquad\qquad V = \frac{m}{\rho}
$$

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Two units" minutes={8}>

$1\,\text{m}^3 = 100\,\text{cm} \times 100\,\text{cm} \times 100\,\text{cm} = 1\,000\,000\,\text{cm}^3$ and $1\,\text{kg} = 1000\,\text{g}$. So:

$$
1\,\text{g/cm}^3 = 1000\,\text{kg/m}^3
$$

Example: water has a density of $1.0\,\text{g/cm}^3 = 1000\,\text{kg/m}^3$.

<div class="table-wrap">

| material | density / (g/cm³) | density / (kg/m³) |
|---|---|---|
| cork | 0.24 | 240 |
| ice | 0.92 | 920 |
| water | 1.0 | 1000 |
| aluminium | 2.7 | 2700 |
| iron | 7.9 | 7900 |
| lead | 11.3 | 11 300 |
| gold | 19.3 | 19 300 |

</div>

<p class="small muted">Typical values at room temperature.</p>

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 20 min, practice 25 min, exit ticket 10 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- Dividing the wrong way ($V/m$).
- Mixing units: g with m³, or kg with cm³.
- Changing g/cm³ to kg/m³ by dividing, or by 100.

**Homework answer:** $m = 19.3 \times 50 = 965\,\text{g} = 0.965\,\text{kg}$.

**Data:** densities are typical values from standard reference tables, rounded. Real samples vary a little (especially wood, cork and oils).

**Language support:**

- Use the phrase "how much mass in each cm³" before the word *density*.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: use g and cm³ only (Levels 1 and 2).
- Stretch: air has a density of about $1.2\,\text{kg/m}^3$. Estimate the mass of the air in your classroom.

</Step>
