<Step kind="learn" title="Mass" minutes={8}>

**Mass** is a measure of the quantity of matter (the amount of "stuff") in an object.

- The unit of mass is the **kilogram (kg)**. We also use grams: $1000\,\text{g} = 1\,\text{kg}$.
- The mass of an object is **the same everywhere**: on the Earth, on the Moon, or in space.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Weight" minutes={10}>

**Weight** is the **gravitational force** on an object that has mass. The Earth pulls everything towards its centre.

- Weight is a **force**, so its unit is the **newton (N)**.
- We measure weight with a **force meter** (a newton meter).
- Weight depends on where you are. On the Moon, gravity is weaker, so your weight is smaller.

<div class="table-wrap">

| | Mass | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| What is it? | the amount of matter | the gravitational force on the object |
| Unit | kilogram (kg) | newton (N) |
| Measured with | a balance | a force meter |
| Same everywhere? | yes | no: it depends on gravity |
| Scalar or vector? | scalar | vector (it points down, towards the centre of the planet) |

</div>

**Be careful:** in everyday English, people say "my weight is 60 kilograms". In physics, this is your **mass**. Your weight is a force, in newtons.

### Comparing with a balance

A **balance** compares the weights of two objects. When the two sides are level, the two weights are equal, so the two masses are also equal.
This works on the Moon too: gravity pulls both sides in the same way.

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Mass and weight of objects" minutes={17}>

**Equipment:** a balance (scale), a force meter (0–10 N), 5 objects (for example a pencil case, a book, a water bottle, a shoe, a bag of sugar).

1. Measure the **mass** of each object with the balance. Write it in kg.
2. Hang each object from the force meter. Measure its **weight** in N.
3. Divide the weight by the mass for each object.

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 18 min, activity 17 min, check 15 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- Using "weight" for mass, as in everyday English ("I weigh 60 kg").
- Thinking mass changes on the Moon, or that objects have no mass in space.
- Thinking a balance gives a different result on the Moon (it compares, so it does not).

**Activity:** weight ÷ mass should be close to 9.8 N/kg for every object. Lesson 2 builds on this result.

**Language support:**

- Put the mass/weight table on the wall for the whole unit.
- Sentence frame: *"Mass is the …, measured in … Weight is the …, measured in …"*

**Differentiation:**

- Support: give the masses already in kg.
- Stretch: an object in orbit around the Earth is "weightless". Is its weight really zero? (No: gravity still acts on it. It is in free fall.)

</Step>
