<Step kind="learn" title="Three methods" minutes={15}>

To find a density, we always need two measurements: the **mass** (with a balance) and the **volume**. Then $\rho = \dfrac{m}{V}$.

### 1. A regular solid (a block)

1. Measure the mass $m$ on a balance.
2. Measure the length, width and height with a ruler.
3. Volume: $V = \text{length} \times \text{width} \times \text{height}$.
4. Calculate $\rho = \dfrac{m}{V}$.

### 2. An irregular solid that sinks: displacement

1. Measure the mass $m$ of the object on a balance.
2. Put some water in a measuring cylinder. Read the volume $V_1$.
3. Put the object in the water carefully. It sinks and pushes the water up (**displacement**). Read the new volume $V_2$.
4. Volume of the object: $V = V_2 - V_1$.
5. Calculate $\rho = \dfrac{m}{V}$.

<WorkedExample
  question="A stone has a mass of 45 g. The water level goes from 40 cm³ to 58 cm³. Calculate the density of the stone."
  given={['$m = 45\\,\\text{g}$', '$V_1 = 40\\,\\text{cm}^3$', '$V_2 = 58\\,\\text{cm}^3$']}
  find="the density $\rho$"
  answer="$\rho = 2.5\,\text{g/cm}^3$"
>

$V = V_2 - V_1 = 58 - 40 = 18\,\text{cm}^3$

$\rho = \dfrac{m}{V} = \dfrac{45}{18} = 2.5\,\text{g/cm}^3$

</WorkedExample>

### 3. A liquid

1. Measure the mass of an empty measuring cylinder: $m_1$.
2. Pour some liquid in. Read the volume $V$.
3. Measure the mass of the cylinder with the liquid: $m_2$.
4. Mass of the liquid: $m = m_2 - m_1$.
5. Calculate $\rho = \dfrac{m}{V}$.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 15 min, practical 20 min, practice 15 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- Using $V_2$ (water + object) as the volume of the object.
- Using the mass of the cylinder + liquid as the mass of the liquid.
- Reading the top of the curved surface in the measuring cylinder.

**Practical tips:** tilt the measuring cylinder and let the stone slide down the side, so no water splashes out. Use a stone that fits easily and sinks. For a precise result with a small stone, use a smaller measuring cylinder (finer scale).

**Answer to question 3:** the balance measures the cylinder too, so you subtract the mass of the empty cylinder.

**Language support:**

- Act out *displace*: push a bottle into a full bowl of water and watch the water rise.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: give the three methods as cards with pictures.
- Stretch: how could you find the density of a cork that **floats**? (Push it under with a thin pin, or attach a sinker and subtract the sinker’s volume.)

**Safety:** clean water spills at once. Glass measuring cylinders can break.

</Step>
