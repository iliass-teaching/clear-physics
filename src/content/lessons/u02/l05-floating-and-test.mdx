<Step kind="starter" title="Float or sink?" minutes={5}>

Your teacher puts objects in a bowl of water: an apple, a coin, a candle, a key, a cork.

Before each one, predict: **float** or **sink**? Write your predictions, then watch.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="The rule" minutes={8}>

Compare the density of the object with the density of the liquid:

- density of the object **less than** the liquid → it **floats**;
- density of the object **more than** the liquid → it **sinks**.

The density of water is $1.0\,\text{g/cm}^3$. So:

- wood (about $0.5\,\text{g/cm}^3$) and ice ($0.92\,\text{g/cm}^3$) float in water;
- iron ($7.9\,\text{g/cm}^3$) sinks.

**Be careful:** a big object does not always sink, and a small object does not always float. Only the **density** matters: a huge tree trunk floats, a small coin sinks.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Liquids that do not mix (Extended)" minutes={7}>

Some liquids do not mix, for example oil and water. The liquid with the **lower density floats on top** of the other one.

<Figure svg={densityColumnSvg()} caption="Three liquids that do not mix. Each object floats on a liquid that is denser than it." width="28rem" />

- Oil ($0.92\,\text{g/cm}^3$) floats on water ($1.0\,\text{g/cm}^3$).
- Water floats on honey ($1.4\,\text{g/cm}^3$).
- The wood floats on the oil. The plastic bead sinks in the water but floats on the honey. The iron nut sinks to the bottom.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** floating from density data (1.4 C3) is Core. Liquids that do not mix (1.4 S4) is Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 15 min, unit test 40 min.

**Starter:** typical results: apple floats, coin sinks, candle floats, key sinks, cork floats.

**Using the test:** questions 1–8 test Core content; 9 and 10 are Extended.

**Common mistakes:**

- "Heavy things sink, light things float." Only density matters.
- Thinking the densest liquid floats on top.

**Homework answer:** the ship and the air inside it together have a mean density less than water.

**Language support:**

- Sentence frame: *"The … floats because its density is less than the density of …"*

**Differentiation:**

- Support: give the density table during the test the first time.
- Stretch: build a density column with honey, water (with food colouring) and oil. Drop in small objects and predict where they stop.

</Step>
