<Step kind="learn" title="Gravitational field strength" minutes={12}>

Around the Earth there is a **gravitational field**: a region where a mass feels a gravitational force.

The **gravitational field strength** $g$ is the **force per unit mass**: the force on each kilogram.

$$
g = \frac{W}{m}
$$

<div class="table-wrap">

| Quantity | Symbol | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| gravitational field strength | $g$ | N/kg |
| weight | $W$ | N |
| mass | $m$ | kg |

</div>

On the Earth, $g = 9.8\,\text{N/kg}$: the Earth pulls on each kilogram with a force of 9.8 N. This is the value you found in the starter.

We can change the equation to find the weight: multiply both sides by $m$.

$$
W = m \times g
$$

<WorkedExample
  question="A suitcase has a mass of 15 kg. Calculate its weight on the Earth."
  given={['$m = 15\\,\\text{kg}$', '$g = 9.8\\,\\text{N/kg}$']}
  find="the weight $W$"
  answer="$W = 147\,\text{N}$"
>

$W = m \times g$

$W = 15 \times 9.8 = 147\,\text{N}$

</WorkedExample>

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="g is also an acceleration" minutes={6}>

In Unit 1, the **acceleration of free fall** near the Earth was $9.8\,\text{m/s}^2$.
It has the same value and the same symbol as the gravitational field strength. This is not a coincidence: they are the **same quantity**.

$$
g = 9.8\,\text{N/kg} = 9.8\,\text{m/s}^2
$$

**Extended:** your weight is the **effect of a gravitational field on your mass**. The stronger the field, the bigger the weight of the same mass.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Weight on other planets" minutes={5}>

Each planet has its own gravitational field strength.

<PlanetTable columns={['g']} />

On the Moon, $g = 1.6\,\text{N/kg}$. So a 60 kg astronaut has a weight of $60 \times 1.6 = 96\,\text{N}$ on the Moon, but $60 \times 9.8 = 588\,\text{N}$ on the Earth. Her mass is 60 kg in both places.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** $g = W/m$ and the link with the acceleration of free fall (1.3 C3) are Core. Weight as the effect of a gravitational field on a mass (1.3 S5) is Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 23 min, practice 22 min, exit ticket 10 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- Giving a weight in kg, or a mass in N.
- Using $g = 9.8\,\text{N/kg}$ for other planets.
- Forgetting to change grams to kilograms before using $W = mg$.

**Language support:**

- Read "N/kg" as "newtons per kilogram" and connect it to "force on each kilogram".

**Differentiation:**

- Support: start with Level 1 and masses in kg only.
- Stretch: why is $g$ on Venus (8.9 N/kg) almost the same as on the Earth? (Venus has a similar mass and size.)

**Data:** values of $g$ from the NASA Planetary Fact Sheet. For the gas planets, NASA gives $g$ at the cloud tops.

</Step>
