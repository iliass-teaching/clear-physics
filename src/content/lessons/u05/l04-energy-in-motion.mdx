Where does it have the most kinetic energy? The most gravitational potential energy?

<Step kind="learn" title="Falling: GPE becomes KE" minutes={12}>

When an object falls (with no air resistance), the energy in its gravitational potential store goes into its kinetic store:

$$
\text{GPE lost} = \text{KE gained} \qquad mg\Delta h = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2
$$

<WorkedExample
  question="A 2.0 kg stone falls 5.0 m. Ignore air resistance. Calculate its speed just before it hits the ground."
  given={['$m = 2.0\\,\\text{kg}$', '$\\Delta h = 5.0\\,\\text{m}$', '$g = 9.8\\,\\text{N/kg}$', 'it starts at rest']}
  find="the speed $v$"
  answer="$v = 9.9\,\text{m/s}$"
>

GPE lost $= mg\Delta h = 2.0 \times 9.8 \times 5.0 = 98\,\text{J}$

KE gained $= 98\,\text{J}$, so $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 2.0 \times v^2 = 98$

$v^2 = 98$, so $v = \sqrt{98} = 9.9\,\text{m/s}$

</WorkedExample>

**Rising** is the opposite: a ball thrown up loses kinetic energy and gains gravitational potential energy. At the top, it stops for a moment: all the KE has become GPE.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Several stages: a roller coaster" minutes={10}>

<Figure svg={rollerCoasterSvg(30, 10, 18)} caption="A roller coaster car starts at rest at A." width="34rem" />

With no friction, the **total** energy (KE + GPE) is the same at A, B and C:

- at A: all GPE (the car is at rest);
- at B: lower down, so less GPE and more KE: the car is fast;
- at C: higher than B, so it slows down again, but it still moves.

**In real life**, friction and air resistance transfer some energy to the internal (thermal) store of the track, the car and the air. So the real speeds are a bit smaller, and the car can never go back up higher than A.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Extended content** (1.7.1 S4–S6). Core students can do the starter and the qualitative part of the roller coaster.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 22 min, table 13 min, practice 15 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Table answers:** total energy 147 000 J everywhere. A: GPE 147 000 J. B: GPE 49 000 J, KE 98 000 J, speed 19.8 m/s. C: GPE 88 200 J, KE 58 800 J, speed 15.3 m/s.

**Homework answer:** $v = \sqrt{2 \times 9.8 \times 2.5} = 7.0\,\text{m/s}$.

**Common mistakes:**

- Using the height of the point instead of the height lost.
- Forgetting that the mass cancels: $v = \sqrt{2g\Delta h}$ does not depend on the mass.
- Thinking a real coaster can rise higher than its start.

**Language support:**

- Sentence frame: *"As the … falls, energy moves from the … store to the … store."*

**Differentiation:**

- Support: complete the table row by row with the class.
- Stretch: on a real coaster, the speed at B is 18 m/s. How much energy was transferred by friction from A to B? ($98\,000 - \tfrac{1}{2} \times 500 \times 18^2 = 17\,000\,\text{J}$.)

</Step>
