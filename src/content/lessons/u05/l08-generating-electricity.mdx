When you switch on a light, the energy comes from a power station far away.

List all the ways you know to generate electricity. Which ones have you seen?

<Step kind="learn" title="Boiler, turbine, generator" minutes={10}>

Many power stations work in the same way:

<Figure svg={powerStationSvg()} caption="A thermal power station." width="38rem" />

1. **Boiler:** a fuel heats water. The water boils and becomes high-pressure steam.
2. **Turbine:** the steam pushes the blades of a turbine, which spins (kinetic store).
3. **Generator:** the turbine turns the generator, which generates electricity.

Coal, oil, gas, biofuels and nuclear fuel all heat water in a boiler. Wind, water and waves turn the turbine **directly**, with no boiler.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Energy resources" minutes={15}>

<div class="table-wrap">

| Resource | Where the energy comes from | How it works |
|---|---|---|
| fossil fuels (coal, oil, gas) | chemical store | burned to heat water in a boiler → turbine → generator |
| biofuels (wood, plant oils, biogas) | chemical store | burned like fossil fuels |
| nuclear fuel (uranium) | nuclear store | nuclear fission heats water in a boiler → turbine → generator |
| geothermal | hot rocks underground | hot rocks heat water to make steam → turbine → generator |
| hydroelectric | gravitational potential store of water behind a dam | water flows down through turbines → generator |
| tides | the rise and fall of the sea | water flows through turbines in a barrage → generator |
| waves | kinetic store of the waves | waves move floats or push air through turbines → generator |
| wind | kinetic store of the air | wind turns the blades of a wind turbine → generator |
| solar cells | light from the Sun | light is transferred directly into electrical energy |
| solar panels | infrared from the Sun | heat water for homes (no electricity) |

</div>

**Extended:**

- **Radiation from the Sun** is the main source of energy for all our resources, **except** geothermal, nuclear and tidal. (Fossil fuels and biofuels come from plants that used sunlight. The Sun heats the air to make wind, and evaporates water that falls as rain for hydroelectric dams.)
- The Sun releases its energy by **nuclear fusion**.
- Scientists are researching how to use nuclear fusion to generate electricity on a large scale.

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Resource cards" minutes={15}>

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** 1.7.3 C1 is Core. The Sun as the main source (S4), fusion in the Sun (S5) and fusion research (S6) are Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 25 min, resource cards 15 min, check 10 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- Thinking the generator makes energy. It transfers energy from the kinetic store to electrical work.
- Mixing up solar cells (electricity) and solar panels (hot water).
- Thinking nuclear power stations use fusion. Today they use fission; fusion is still being researched.

**Language support:**

- Show a bicycle dynamo or a small hand generator: turning it lights a lamp.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: give the flow diagrams with gaps to fill.
- Stretch: why is tidal energy not from the Sun? (Tides are mainly caused by the Moon’s gravity.)

**Homework:** the answer depends on the country. For example, natural gas generates more than half of Thailand’s electricity (2024 data). Check a recent national source.

</Step>
