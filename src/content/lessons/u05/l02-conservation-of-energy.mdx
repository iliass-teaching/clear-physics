<Step kind="starter" title="Where did it go?" minutes={5}>

A ball is dropped. It bounces, but each bounce is lower. In the end it stops.

Has the energy disappeared? Where did it go?

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Energy is conserved" minutes={10}>

The **principle of conservation of energy**:

> Energy cannot be created or destroyed. It can only be transferred from one store to another.

So the **total** energy is always the same. In the starter, the energy of the ball is not destroyed: at each bounce, some is transferred to the internal (thermal) store of the ball, the floor and the air, and some is carried away by sound.

Energy that ends up spread out in the surroundings, usually as heating, is **wasted**: we cannot use it again easily. We say it is **dissipated**.

<Figure svg={flowSvg(['chemical store|(petrol)', 'kinetic store|of the car', 'internal store|of the surroundings'])} caption="A car journey: in the end, all the energy heats the surroundings (brakes, engine, air)." width="34rem" />

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Sankey diagrams (Extended)" minutes={12}>

A **Sankey diagram** shows the energy transfers in a device. The **width** of each arrow shows the amount of energy.

<Figure svg={sankeySvg('electrical input', 100, [{ label: 'light', value: 10, useful: true }, { label: 'heating', value: 90 }])} caption="A filament lamp: for every 100 J in, only 10 J become light." width="36rem" />

- The arrow going straight on is the **useful** output.
- The arrows that bend down are the **wasted** outputs.
- Conservation of energy: input = useful + wasted. Here: $100 = 10 + 90$.

A more complex example, with several stages: a power station burns fuel (chemical store) to heat water, the steam turns a turbine (kinetic store), and the generator gives electrical work. At each stage, some energy is wasted by heating. The Sankey diagram has a wasted arrow at each stage.

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Draw Sankey diagrams" minutes={15}>

On squared paper, draw a Sankey diagram for each device. Use 1 square = 10 J.

1. An LED lamp: 100 J in → 40 J light, 60 J heating.
2. A kettle: 2000 J in → 1800 J into the water, 200 J heating the kettle and the air. (Use 1 square = 200 J.)
3. An electric motor: 500 J in → 350 J kinetic, 100 J heating, 50 J sound. (Use 1 square = 50 J.)

For each one, check that **input = total output**.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** conservation of energy with simple flow diagrams (1.7.1 C3) is Core. Complex multi-stage examples and Sankey diagrams (1.7.1 S6) are Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 22 min, Sankey drawing 15 min, check 13 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Exit ticket answer:** 1200 − 1000 − 50 = 150 J.

**Common mistakes:**

- "Energy is used up" or "lost". Insist on "transferred" and "wasted".
- Drawing Sankey arrows with widths that do not match the numbers.
- Forgetting one of the wasted outputs.

**Language support:**

- *Dissipated* = spread out into the surroundings.
- Sentence frame: *"… J goes into the … store. The rest, … J, is wasted by …"*

**Differentiation:**

- Support: give Sankey diagrams to complete, with the input already drawn.
- Stretch: draw a Sankey diagram with two stages for a power station: 1000 J fuel → 400 J to the turbine (600 J wasted in the boiler) → 350 J electrical (50 J wasted in the generator).

</Step>
