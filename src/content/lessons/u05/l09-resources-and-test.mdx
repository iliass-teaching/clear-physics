<Step kind="learn" title="Five ways to compare" minutes={12}>

- **Renewability:** will it run out? (renewable or non-renewable)
- **Availability:** can every country use it, or only some places?
- **Reliability:** does it work all the time, or only sometimes (for example, only when it is windy)?
- **Scale:** how much electricity can one power station make?
- **Environmental impact:** does it release carbon dioxide (climate change) or pollution, or damage habitats?

<div class="table-wrap">

| resource | renewable? | reliable? | main advantages | main disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fossil fuels | no | yes | large scale, can be switched on when needed | release CO₂ (climate change) and pollution |
| nuclear | no | yes | large scale, no CO₂ while running | radioactive waste, risk of accidents, expensive to build |
| biofuels | yes | yes | plants take in CO₂ as they grow | need a lot of farmland |
| hydroelectric | yes | yes | large scale, can start quickly | floods valleys and habitats, only in hilly areas with rain |
| tides | yes | yes (predictable) | no CO₂ while running | few suitable places, can change habitats in estuaries |
| waves | yes | no | no CO₂ while running | small scale, depends on the weather |
| wind | yes | no | no CO₂ while running | depends on the wind, noise, changes the landscape |
| solar cells | yes | no | no CO₂ while running, good for remote places | no electricity at night, less on cloudy days |
| geothermal | yes | yes | no CO₂ from fuel | only in a few places (volcanic areas) |

</div>

<Step kind="do" title="Power for an island" minutes={15}>

A tropical island has: strong sunshine, steady winds, a coast with big waves, no rivers, no coal, oil or gas. Today it buys diesel by ship to make electricity.

In groups, choose **two** resources for the island. Write a short report:

1. Your two choices.
2. Two advantages of each, using the five ways to compare.
3. One problem, and how you would solve it (for example, at night or when there is no wind).

*Useful phrases: "We chose … because …", "One disadvantage is …", "To solve this, …"*

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Timing:** learn 12 min, island task 15 min, unit test 33 min.

**About the table:** it is a simplified summary for the exam (1.7.3 C2). Real data (costs, emissions over the whole life of a power station) are more complex and change over time.

**Island task:** good answers use solar cells (strong sunshine) and wind (steady winds), with batteries or waves for the night. Reward justification more than the "right" answer.

**Using the test:** questions 1, 2, 5, 6, 8 and 9 test Core content; 3, 4, 7 and 10 are Extended.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: give the island task as a table to fill.
- Stretch: suggest why a country might keep some gas power stations even with a lot of wind and solar power. (They can be switched on quickly when there is no wind or sun.)

</Step>
