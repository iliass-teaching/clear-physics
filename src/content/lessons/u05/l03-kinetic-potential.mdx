<Step kind="learn" title="Kinetic energy" minutes={12}>

The energy in the kinetic store of a moving object:

$$
E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2
$$

<div class="table-wrap">

| Quantity | Symbol | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| kinetic energy | $E_k$ | J |
| mass | $m$ | kg |
| speed | $v$ | m/s |

</div>

Only the **speed** is squared. So if the speed doubles, the kinetic energy becomes $2^2 = 4$ times bigger.

<WorkedExample
  question="A car of mass 1000 kg moves at 20 m/s. Calculate its kinetic energy."
  given={['$m = 1000\\,\\text{kg}$', '$v = 20\\,\\text{m/s}$']}
  find="the kinetic energy $E_k$"
  answer="$E_k = 200\,000\,\text{J} = 200\,\text{kJ}$"
>

$E_k = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 1000 \times 20^2$

$E_k = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 1000 \times 400 = 200\,000\,\text{J}$

</WorkedExample>

**Tip:** square the speed first, then multiply.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Gravitational potential energy" minutes={12}>

When an object is lifted, the energy in its gravitational potential store increases:

$$
\Delta E_p = mg\Delta h
$$

- $\Delta E_p$: change in gravitational potential energy (J)
- $m$: mass (kg)
- $g$: gravitational field strength ($9.8\,\text{N/kg}$ on the Earth)
- $\Delta h$: change in height (m)

<WorkedExample
  question="A student of mass 50 kg climbs stairs 6.0 m high. Calculate her gain in gravitational potential energy."
  given={['$m = 50\\,\\text{kg}$', '$\\Delta h = 6.0\\,\\text{m}$', '$g = 9.8\\,\\text{N/kg}$']}
  find="$\Delta E_p$"
  answer="$\Delta E_p = 2940\,\text{J}$"
>

$\Delta E_p = mg\Delta h = 50 \times 9.8 \times 6.0$

$\Delta E_p = 2940\,\text{J}$

</WorkedExample>

### Finding a speed or a height

$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2E_k}{m}} \qquad\qquad \Delta h = \frac{\Delta E_p}{mg}
$$

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Extended content** (1.7.1 S4, S5).

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 24 min, practice 23 min, exit ticket 8 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- Forgetting the ½, or squaring $\tfrac{1}{2}m$ as well as $v$.
- Using $g = 10$ when the question gives 9.8 (follow the question).
- Using the height in cm.

**Language support:**

- Read $\Delta h$ as "change in height". Say the units with every number.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: start with Level 2 ($mg\Delta h$, no squares).
- Stretch: a 0.06 kg tennis ball is served at 50 m/s. From what height would it need to fall to get the same kinetic energy (ignore air resistance)? ($\Delta h = \dfrac{v^2}{2g} \approx 128\,\text{m}$.)

</Step>
