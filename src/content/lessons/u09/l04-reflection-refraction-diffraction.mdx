<Step kind="starter" title="Around the corner" minutes={5}>

Your friend is in the corridor, around the corner. You cannot see your friend, but you can hear them. Why can sound go around the corner, but light cannot (or only very little)?

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="The ripple tank" minutes={8}>

<Figure svg={rippleTankSvg()} caption="A ripple tank, seen from the side." width="32rem" />

- A **motor** makes a bar **vibrate** on the water: it makes straight waves.
- A **lamp** above the tank shines through the water. Each crest acts like a lens: we see **bright lines** on the screen below. These lines show the **wavefronts**.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Reflection and refraction" minutes={14}>

**Reflection:** a wave hits a surface and comes back.

<Figure svg={reflectionWavesSvg()} caption="Reflection at a flat (plane) barrier." width="28rem" />

- In a ripple tank: put a flat metal barrier in the water.
- The waves come back at the **same angle**. The wavelength, the frequency and the speed do not change.

**Refraction:** a wave **changes speed** when it goes into a different medium. So it can **change direction**.

<Figure svg={refractionWavesSvg()} caption="Refraction: from deep water to shallow water." width="30rem" />

- In a ripple tank: put a glass plate in the tank, so the water above it is **shallower**.
- Water waves are **slower** in shallow water. So the **wavelength gets shorter**.
- The **frequency does not change** (the same number of waves arrive each second).
- If the waves arrive at an angle, they **change direction**.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Diffraction" minutes={14}>

**Diffraction:** a wave **spreads out** when it goes through a **gap** or past an **edge**. The wavelength, the frequency and the speed do not change.

<Figure svg={diffractionGapSvg()} caption="Diffraction through a gap." width="34rem" />

<Figure svg={diffractionEdgeSvg()} caption="Diffraction at an edge." width="24rem" />

In a ripple tank: put two barriers with a gap between them, or one barrier with an edge.

**Extended: what changes the diffraction?**

- **Gap size:** the waves spread out the most when the gap is about the **same size as the wavelength**. A **wide** gap (much bigger than the wavelength) gives **only a little** spreading.
- **Wavelength:** waves with a **longer** wavelength spread out **more** (through the same gap, or at an edge).

**Back to the starter:** sound has a wavelength of about 1 m, like the size of a door. So it diffracts a lot. Light has a very, very short wavelength (less than a millionth of a metre), so it diffracts very little at a door.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** reflection, refraction, diffraction and the ripple tank (3.1 C7, C8) are Core. The effect of gap size and wavelength (S9, S10) is Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 36 min, check 14 min, exit ticket 5 min. If you have a ripple tank, show each effect as you teach it.

**Ripple tank tips:** use very shallow water (about 5 mm), and a thin glass or plastic plate for the shallow region. Dim the room lights. Use a low frequency at first.

**Common mistakes:**

- Drawing a shorter or longer wavelength after diffraction or reflection. The wavelength does not change.
- Thinking that the frequency changes in refraction.
- "A wider gap gives more diffraction."

**Language support:**

- *re-flect* = bend back; *re-fract* = break (change direction); *dif-fract* = break apart, spread.
- Sentence frame: *"When the waves go through a gap that is about the same size as the wavelength, they …"*

**Differentiation:**

- Support: three cards with the names and three pictures to match.
- Stretch: why can you receive long-wave radio behind a hill, but not some higher-frequency signals?

**Safety:** water and electricity: keep the lamp and the motor dry, and wipe up spills.

</Step>
