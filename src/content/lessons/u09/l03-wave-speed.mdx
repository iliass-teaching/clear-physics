<Step kind="learn" title="The wave equation" minutes={12}>

In one second, $f$ waves pass a point. Each wave is $\lambda$ long. So in one second the wave travels $f \times \lambda$:

$$
v = f\lambda
$$

<div class="table-wrap">

| Quantity | Symbol | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| wave speed | $v$ | m/s |
| frequency | $f$ | Hz |
| wavelength | $\lambda$ | m |

</div>

To find $f$ or $\lambda$, change the subject:

$$
f = \frac{v}{\lambda} \qquad \lambda = \frac{v}{f}
$$

**Units first:** 1 kHz = 1000 Hz; 1 MHz = 1 000 000 Hz = $10^6$ Hz; 1 cm = 0.01 m.

<WorkedExample
  question="A sound wave has a frequency of 680 Hz. The speed of sound in air is 340 m/s. Calculate its wavelength."
  given={['$f = 680\\,\\text{Hz}$', '$v = 340\\,\\text{m/s}$']}
  find="the wavelength $\lambda$"
  answer="$\lambda = 0.5\,\text{m}$"
>

$v = f\lambda$, so $\lambda = \dfrac{v}{f}$

$\lambda = \dfrac{340}{680}$

$\lambda = 0.5\,\text{m}$

</WorkedExample>

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 12 min, simulation 13 min, practice 25 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Values used:** speed of sound in air 340 m/s (the syllabus, topic 3.4, gives a range of about 330–350 m/s); speed of electromagnetic waves $3.0 \times 10^8$ m/s (syllabus 3.3).

**Common mistakes:**

- Dividing instead of multiplying.
- Wavelength in cm, frequency in kHz or MHz.
- Thinking that a higher frequency makes the wave faster. In one medium, the speed is (nearly) fixed; $f$ and $\lambda$ change together.

**Language support:**

- Read $v = f\lambda$ as "speed equals frequency times wavelength".

**Differentiation:**

- Support: level 1 only, with the three forms of the equation written on the board.
- Stretch: level 3 (reading a wave graph; counting waves).

</Step>
