<Step kind="learn" title="The parts of a wave" minutes={14}>

<Figure svg={waveDiagramSvg()} caption="The parts of a wave." width="34rem" />

- **Crest** (or **peak**): the highest point of the wave.
- **Trough**: the lowest point of the wave.
- **Amplitude**: the distance from the **middle line** (the rest position) to a **crest** (or to a trough). It is **not** from a trough to a crest.
- **Wavelength** $\lambda$ (the Greek letter *lambda*): the length of **one full wave**, for example from one crest to the next crest. Unit: metre (m).
- **Frequency** $f$: the number of waves that pass a point **each second**. Unit: **hertz** (Hz). 1 Hz = 1 wave per second.
- **Wave speed** $v$: the distance the wave travels each second. Unit: m/s.

A bigger amplitude means the wave carries more energy.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Wavefronts" minutes={8}>

When we look at water waves **from above**, we draw each crest as a line. These lines are called **wavefronts**.

<Figure svg={wavefrontsSvg()} caption="Wavefronts. The distance between two wavefronts is one wavelength." width="30rem" />

- The distance between two wavefronts next to each other is **one wavelength**.
- The wave travels at right angles to the wavefronts.

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Measure the wavelength" minutes={15}>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 22 min, simulation 15 min, check 13 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- Amplitude measured from trough to crest.
- Wavelength measured from a crest to the next trough (half a wavelength).
- Mixing up the axes: on a displacement–**distance** graph, the horizontal axis gives the wavelength. (On a displacement–time graph it would give the time for one wave; this is not needed in this syllabus.)

**Language support:**

- $\lambda$ is called *lambda*. Write it in one stroke, so that it does not look like a letter y.
- *crest*: the top, like the crest of a hill. *trough*: a long, low container for animals' water.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: a labelled wave diagram to copy before the questions.
- Stretch: two waves have the same frequency. Wave A has twice the wavelength of wave B. Which is faster?

</Step>
