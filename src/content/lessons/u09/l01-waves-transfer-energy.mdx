<Step kind="learn" title="What is a wave?" minutes={10}>

A **wave** is a **vibration** that travels. It **transfers energy** from one place to another, but it does **not transfer matter**.

- A **water wave** moves across a pond. A leaf on the water only goes up and down: it does not move with the wave.
- A wave on a **rope**: each part of the rope moves up and down. The wave moves along the rope.
- A **sound** wave: the air particles vibrate backwards and forwards. The air does not travel from the speaker to your ear, but the energy does.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Two types of wave" minutes={12}>

**Transverse waves:** the vibration is at **right angles** (90°) to the direction the wave travels.

<Figure svg={transverseSvg()} caption="A transverse wave on a rope." width="32rem" />

Examples: **water waves**, **light** and all **electromagnetic** waves, seismic **S-waves** (secondary waves in an earthquake).

**Longitudinal waves:** the vibration is **parallel** to the direction the wave travels (along the same line).

<Figure svg={longitudinalSvg()} caption="A longitudinal wave on a spring." width="32rem" />

- **Compression:** the coils (or particles) are close together.
- **Rarefaction:** the coils (or particles) are far apart.

Examples: **sound** waves, seismic **P-waves** (primary waves in an earthquake).

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Make waves" minutes={18}>

**With a long spring (a "slinky"):** work in pairs, on the floor. Stretch the spring between you (about 3 m).

1. Move one end quickly **from side to side**. Draw the wave you see. Which type is it?
2. Push one end quickly **forwards and backwards**, along the spring. Draw the wave. Which type is it? Find a compression and a rarefaction.
3. Tie a small piece of ribbon to one coil. Make a wave again. Does the ribbon travel along the spring?

**In the simulation:** the red point is like the ribbon.

2. Change to **Longitudinal**. How does the red coil move now?

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 22 min, slinky and simulation 18 min, check 10 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Syllabus wording:** the syllabus says water waves, electromagnetic waves and S-waves *can be modelled as* transverse, and sound and P-waves *can be modelled as* longitudinal. (Real water waves are a little more complex: the water particles move in small circles.)

**Note:** the words *compression* and *rarefaction* are in the syllabus under sound (3.4 S10, Extended). We introduce them here because they help all students to picture a longitudinal wave; Core students are not tested on them.

**Common mistakes:**

- "The wave carries the water with it."
- Mixing up *parallel* and *at right angles*. Use arms: one arm for the vibration, one arm for the direction of travel.
- Drawing a longitudinal wave as a curve.

**Language support:**

- *transverse* = across; *longitudinal* = along (like *longitude* lines on a map, which go along the Earth).
- *parallel* = along the same line, like railway tracks.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: a matching task: picture → transverse or longitudinal.
- Stretch: in an earthquake, P-waves arrive before S-waves. What does this tell you about their speeds?

**Safety:** slinky springs can snap back and hurt. Do not let go of a stretched spring.

</Step>
