<Step kind="learn" title="The turning effect of a force" minutes={12}>

A force can make an object **turn** around a fixed point, the **pivot**.

The turning effect of a force is its **moment**. The moment is bigger when:

- the **force** is bigger;
- the force acts **further from the pivot**.

$$
\text{moment} = \text{force} \times \text{perpendicular distance from the pivot}
$$

The unit of moment is the **newton metre (N m)**.

**Perpendicular distance** means the distance measured at right angles to the force. When you push straight (at right angles) on a door, it is just the distance from the hinge to your hand.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Everyday example | Pivot | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| door handle | the hinges | the handle is far from the hinges: a big moment with a small force |
| spanner | the nut | a long spanner gives a big moment |
| see-saw | the middle | a light child far from the middle can balance a heavy child near it |
| wheelbarrow | the wheel | long handles make the load easy to lift |

</div>

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Calculating a moment" minutes={8}>

<WorkedExample
  question="A mechanic pulls on a spanner with a force of 60 N, at right angles, 25 cm from the nut. Calculate the moment."
  given={['$F = 60\\,\\text{N}$', '$d = 25\\,\\text{cm} = 0.25\\,\\text{m}$']}
  find="the moment of the force"
  answer="$15\,\text{N m}$"
>

$\text{moment} = F \times d = 60 \times 0.25$

$\text{moment} = 15\,\text{N m}$

</WorkedExample>

**Tip:** change cm to m first, so the answer is in N m.

A moment can be **clockwise** (like the hands of a clock) or **anticlockwise** (the other way).

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 20 min, activity 10 min, practice 20 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- Using the distance in cm and giving the answer in N m.
- Using a distance that is not perpendicular to the force.
- Confusing the unit N m (moment) with N/m (spring constant).

**Activity:** the moment needed to start opening the door is about the same at every distance: a smaller distance needs a bigger force.

**Language support:**

- Show *clockwise* and *anticlockwise* with a real clock.
- In everyday English, *moment* means a short time. In physics, it is a turning effect.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: use distances already in metres.
- Stretch: why is it hard to open a door if you push near the hinges at an angle? (The perpendicular distance is even smaller.)

</Step>
