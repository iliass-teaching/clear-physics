<Step kind="learn" title="Load and extension" minutes={8}>

The **load** is the force that stretches the spring (for example the weight of the masses).

The **extension** is how much longer the spring becomes:

$$
\text{extension} = \text{new length} - \text{original length}
$$

**Be careful:** the extension is not the length of the spring.

<Figure svg={springSvg()} caption="Measuring the length of a spring with a load." width="26rem" />

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Spring practical" minutes={20}>

**Equipment:** clamp stand, spring, mass hanger and 100 g masses, metre rule, pointer (optional), safety glasses.

A 100 g mass has a weight of about 1 N.

1. Measure the original length of the spring, with no load.
2. Add one 100 g mass. Measure the new length.
3. Add masses one by one, up to 600 g. Measure the length each time.
4. Calculate the extension for each load.
5. Plot a graph of **load / N** (up) against **extension / cm** (across).

<Step kind="learn" title="Reading the graph" minutes={8}>

For a spring, the first part of the graph is a **straight line through the origin**: the extension is **proportional** to the load. Twice the load gives twice the extension.

<Graph
  xLabel="extension / cm" yLabel="load / N"
  xMax={16} yMax={6} xStep={2} yStep={1}
  points={[[0, 0], [2, 1], [4, 2], [6, 3], [8, 4], [10, 5], [12.5, 5.6], [15, 5.9]]} curve
  labels={[{ x: 10, y: 5.4, text: 'P' }]}
  caption="P is the limit of proportionality. After P, the line is not straight."
/>

**Extended:** the point where the line stops being straight is the **limit of proportionality** (P).

On the straight part, the **spring constant** $k$ is the force per unit extension:

$$
k = \frac{F}{x}
$$

A stiff spring has a big spring constant. The unit is N/m (or N/cm).

<WorkedExample
  question="Extended: use the graph. A load of 4 N gives an extension of 8 cm. Calculate the spring constant in N/m."
  given={['$F = 4\\,\\text{N}$', '$x = 8\\,\\text{cm} = 0.08\\,\\text{m}$']}
  find="the spring constant $k$"
  answer="$k = 50\,\text{N/m}$"
>

$k = \dfrac{F}{x} = \dfrac{4}{0.08}$

$k = 50\,\text{N/m}$

</WorkedExample>

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** load–extension graphs and the experiment (1.5.1 C2) are Core. The spring constant (S9) and the limit of proportionality (S10) are Extended. The syllabus says an understanding of the **elastic limit** is not required.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 16 min, practical 20 min, practice 14 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- Plotting the length instead of the extension.
- Using cm with N/m (forgetting to change cm to m).
- Thinking the line is always straight.

**Practical tips:** $100\,\text{g}$ has a weight of $0.98\,\text{N}$, which we round to 1 N. Use a pointer taped to the bottom of the spring to read the scale.

**Language support:**

- Write *extension = new length − original length* on the board.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: give a printed grid with the axes already labelled.
- Stretch: add masses past the limit of proportionality (with a spare spring). Does the spring go back to its original length?

**Safety:** wear safety glasses. Do not overload the spring: it can snap or fly off. Keep feet away from the falling masses.

</Step>
