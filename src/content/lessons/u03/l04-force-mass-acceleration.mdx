<Step kind="learn" title="F = ma" minutes={12}>

A resultant force makes an object accelerate. The acceleration depends on:

- the **force**: a bigger force gives a bigger acceleration;
- the **mass**: a bigger mass gives a smaller acceleration (for the same force).

$$
F = m \times a
$$

<div class="table-wrap">

| Quantity | Symbol | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| resultant force | $F$ | N |
| mass | $m$ | kg |
| acceleration | $a$ | m/s² |

</div>

One newton is the force that gives a mass of 1 kg an acceleration of $1\,\text{m/s}^2$.

The resultant force and the acceleration are **in the same direction**. A backwards resultant force gives a backwards acceleration: the object slows down.

<WorkedExample
  question="A car of mass 1200 kg accelerates at 2.5 m/s². Calculate the resultant force on the car."
  given={['$m = 1200\\,\\text{kg}$', '$a = 2.5\\,\\text{m/s}^2$']}
  find="the resultant force $F$"
  answer="$F = 3000\,\text{N}$"
>

$F = m \times a = 1200 \times 2.5$

$F = 3000\,\text{N}$

</WorkedExample>

To find $a$ or $m$, change the equation:

$$
a = \frac{F}{m} \qquad\qquad m = \frac{F}{a}
$$

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Resultant force first" minutes={8}>

$F$ in $F = ma$ is the **resultant** force. If there are several forces, find the resultant first.

<WorkedExample
  question="A cyclist and her bike have a mass of 80 kg. The driving force is 300 N and the total resistive force is 140 N. Calculate the acceleration."
  given={['$m = 80\\,\\text{kg}$', 'driving force $= 300\\,\\text{N}$', 'resistive force $= 140\\,\\text{N}$']}
  find="the acceleration $a$"
  answer="$a = 2\,\text{m/s}^2$"
>

Resultant force: $F = 300 - 140 = 160\,\text{N}$

$a = \dfrac{F}{m} = \dfrac{160}{80} = 2\,\text{m/s}^2$

</WorkedExample>

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**This lesson is Extended content** (1.5.1 S11). Core students can do the starter and the trolley data task (shape of the graph only).

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 20 min, data task 10 min, practice 20 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- Using the driving force instead of the resultant force.
- Using grams instead of kilograms.
- Thinking a moving object must have a forward resultant force.

**Data task answers:** a straight line through the origin, so $a$ is proportional to $F$. $a = 1.0 / 0.50 = 2.0\,\text{m/s}^2$. The point at 1.5 N (2.9 instead of 3.0) is a little low, probably because of friction or a reading error.

**Language support:**

- Sentence frame: *"The resultant force is … N, so the acceleration is …"*

**Differentiation:**

- Support: give $F = ma$ and its two other forms on a card.
- Stretch: a lift of mass 800 kg accelerates upwards at $0.5\,\text{m/s}^2$. Calculate the tension in the cable. (Weight 7840 N, resultant 400 N, so tension 8240 N.)

</Step>
