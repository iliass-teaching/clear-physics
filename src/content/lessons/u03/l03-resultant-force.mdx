<Step kind="learn" title="Adding forces along a line" minutes={10}>

The **resultant force** is the single force that has the same effect as all the forces together.

- Forces in the **same direction**: **add** them.
- Forces in **opposite directions**: **subtract** the smaller from the bigger. The resultant acts in the direction of the bigger force.

<Figure svg={forceDiagramSvg([{ dir: 'right', size: 60, label: '80 N' }, { dir: 'left', size: 35, label: '30 N' }])} caption="80 N to the right and 30 N to the left: the resultant is 50 N to the right." width="26rem" />

<WorkedExample
  question="A boat has a driving force of 1200 N forwards. The water pushes back with a drag of 900 N. Calculate the resultant force."
  given={['$1200\\,\\text{N}$ forwards', '$900\\,\\text{N}$ backwards']}
  find="the resultant force"
  answer="$300\,\text{N}$ forwards"
>

The forces act in opposite directions: subtract.

$R = 1200 - 900 = 300\,\text{N}$, forwards (the direction of the bigger force)

</WorkedExample>

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Balanced and unbalanced forces" minutes={10}>

When the resultant force is **zero**, the forces are **balanced**. Then the object:

- **stays at rest**, if it is at rest; or
- **keeps moving at constant speed in a straight line**, if it is moving.

**Be careful:** a moving object does **not** need a resultant force to keep moving. On ice, a puck slides a long way because there is almost no friction.

When there **is** a resultant force, the forces are **unbalanced**. The velocity of the object changes: it speeds up, slows down or changes direction.

<div class="table-wrap">

| resultant force | the object at rest | the moving object |
|---|---|---|
| zero (balanced) | stays at rest | constant speed in a straight line |
| forwards | starts to move forwards | speeds up |
| backwards | starts to move backwards | slows down |

</div>

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 20 min, trolley 10 min, practice 20 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- "A moving object needs a forward force to keep moving." With no resultant force it keeps moving at constant velocity.
- Giving the size of the resultant without its direction.
- Adding forces in opposite directions.

**Link:** this is Newton’s first law. The name is not needed for the exam, but the idea (1.5.1 C4) is.

**Language support:**

- Sentence frame: *"The forces are balanced/unbalanced, so the object …"*

**Differentiation:**

- Support: use Level 1 with diagrams only.
- Stretch: why does a parachute make a skydiver slow down, even though they are still falling? (Air resistance becomes bigger than the weight: the resultant acts upwards.)

**Starter answer:** 500 N each way: the rope does not move. 600 N against 500 N: resultant 100 N towards team B.

</Step>
