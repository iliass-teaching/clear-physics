<Step kind="learn" title="Forces around us" minutes={10}>

A **force** is a push or a pull. We measure forces in **newtons (N)**.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Force | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| weight | the Earth pulls objects down | an apple falls from a tree |
| friction | acts between surfaces, against motion | brakes slow a bicycle |
| air resistance (drag) | acts against motion through air | a parachute slows a skydiver |
| tension | the pull in a rope or a string | a rope holds a lamp |
| contact force | a surface pushes back on an object | a table holds up a book |
| magnetic force | magnets attract or repel | a magnet picks up a nail |

</div>

A force is a **vector**: it has a size and a direction. We draw a force as an **arrow**:

- the arrow points in the direction of the force;
- a longer arrow means a bigger force;
- we write the size next to the arrow.

<Figure svg={forceDiagramSvg([{ dir: 'up', size: 50, label: 'contact force 20 N' }, { dir: 'down', size: 50, label: 'weight 20 N' }])} caption="A book on a table: two forces of the same size, in opposite directions." width="24rem" />

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="What can a force do?" minutes={10}>

A force can:

1. **change the shape or the size** of an object: squash a sponge, stretch a spring, bend a ruler;
2. **change the speed** of an object: start it moving, speed it up, slow it down, stop it;
3. **change the direction** of motion: a bat hits a ball back, a car turns a corner.

Changes 2 and 3 are both changes of **velocity** (speed in a direction). A **resultant force** can change the velocity of an object by changing its speed, its direction, or both.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 20 min, force circus 15 min, check 15 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- "A moving object needs a force to keep moving." This is corrected in Lesson 3.
- Drawing arrows of the same length for forces of different sizes.
- Forgetting the contact force (normal force) from a surface.

**Language support:**

- The word *force* is also used in everyday English ("they forced him"). In physics it only means a push or a pull.
- Mime each force before writing its name.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: give the force names on cards with pictures.
- Stretch: a satellite moves in a circle at constant speed. Is there a force on it? (Yes: its direction changes. See Lesson 6.)

**Safety:** at station 6, use a soft ball.

</Step>
