<Step kind="learn" title="Light changes speed" minutes={13}>

Light travels more **slowly** in glass and water than in air. When light goes from one material into another at an angle, it **changes direction**. This is **refraction**.

<Figure svg={glassBlockSvg()} caption="A ray of light through a rectangular glass block." width="30rem" />

- **Air → glass** (it slows down): the ray bends **towards** the normal. The angle of refraction is **smaller** than the angle of incidence.
- **Glass → air** (it speeds up): the ray bends **away from** the normal.
- A ray along the normal ($i = 0^\circ$) goes **straight on**: it does not bend.
- With a rectangular block, the ray that comes out is **parallel** to the ray that went in.

The **angle of refraction** is the angle between the refracted ray and the **normal**.

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Glass blocks" minutes={20}>

**Equipment:** a ray box with a single slit, a rectangular glass block, a semicircular block and a triangular prism, a protractor, white paper.

1. Draw around the rectangular block. Remove it and draw a normal near one end of the long side.
2. Put the block back. Shine a ray at the normal, at $i = 30^\circ$. Mark the ray that comes out with two crosses.
3. Remove the block. Join the points to draw the ray inside the glass. Measure the angle of refraction.
4. Repeat for 0°, 20°, 40°, 50°, 60°.
5. Now use the **semicircular** block and the **triangular** prism. Draw the path of a ray through each one.

**Questions:**

1. Is the angle of refraction always smaller than the angle of incidence (air → glass)?
2. What happens when $i = 0^\circ$?
3. In the simulation, compare glass, water and diamond. Which bends the light the most?

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 13 min, experiment 20 min, check 17 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Syllabus note:** 3.2.2 C3 is limited to what happens at the boundary between two materials. The refractive index and the equation $n = \dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r}$ come in the next lesson (Extended).

**Common mistakes:**

- Bending the ray the wrong way. Use the idea: "slower → towards the normal".
- Measuring angles from the surface.
- Drawing the ray inside the block without joining the two points.

**Language support:**

- *refract* comes from Latin "to break": the ray looks broken at the surface.
- *transparent* = light can go through it.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: give the block outline with the normal and the incident ray already drawn.
- Stretch: use your results to plot $\sin i$ against $\sin r$. What shape is the graph? (Next lesson.)

**Safety:** glass blocks have sharp edges and are heavy; ray boxes get hot.

</Step>
