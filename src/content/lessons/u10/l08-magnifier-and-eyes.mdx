<Step kind="learn" title="The magnifying glass" minutes={14}>

When the object is **between F and the lens**, the rays leave the lens **spreading out** (diverging). They never meet. But if we follow them **backwards** (dashed lines), they seem to come from a point behind the object.

<Figure svg={lensVirtualImageSvg()} caption="The object is closer than F: the image is virtual, upright and enlarged." width="36rem" />

The image is:

- **virtual** (on the same side as the object; it cannot be shown on a screen);
- **upright**;
- **enlarged**.

This is how a **magnifying glass** works: hold the lens **close to the object** (closer than its focal length) and look through it.

**Ray diagram (two rays from the top of the object):**

1. Parallel to the axis → after the lens, through F. Extend it backwards (dashed).
2. Through the centre → straight on. Extend it backwards (dashed).
3. The image is where the two dashed lines meet.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Correcting sight" minutes={14}>

In a healthy eye, the eye lens makes a sharp image on the **retina** (at the back of the eye).

<Figure svg={eyeSightSvg()} caption="Short sight and long sight, and the lenses that correct them." width="36rem" />

**Short sight** (you can see near things clearly, but not far things):

- The eye bends the light **too much**: the image of a far object forms **in front of** the retina.
- Correction: a **diverging** lens. It spreads the rays a little before they enter the eye, so the image moves back onto the retina.

**Long sight** (you can see far things clearly, but not near things):

- The eye does not bend the light **enough**: the image of a near object would form **behind** the retina.
- Correction: a **converging** lens. It bends the rays a little before they enter the eye, so the image moves forward onto the retina.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Extended only** (3.2.3 S6–S8). Core students can use this lesson to practise real-image ray diagrams with the simulation.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, magnifying glass 14 min, simulation 10 min, sight 14 min, check 12 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**About the eye diagram:** it is simplified. In a real eye, most of the bending happens at the cornea (the front surface), not only in the lens.

**Common mistakes:**

- Swapping the corrections (short sight → diverging; long sight → converging). Memory help: short sight = the light is focused "too short".
- Drawing the virtual image on the wrong side of the lens.
- Solid lines for the backward extensions: they must be dashed.

**Language support:**

- *short-sighted* = you can only see things that are a short distance away. *long-sighted* = you can only see things far away.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: give the eye diagrams; students label "in front of / behind the retina" and choose the lens.
- Stretch: why does a magnifying glass with a shorter focal length magnify more?

**Safety:** never look at the Sun through a lens.

</Step>
