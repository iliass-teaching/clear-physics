<Step kind="learn" title="Refractive index" minutes={14}>

The **refractive index** $n$ of a material tells us how much it slows light down:

$$
n = \frac{\text{speed of light in air (or a vacuum)}}{\text{speed of light in the material}}
$$

It is a **ratio** of two speeds, so it has **no unit**. A bigger $n$ means slower light and more bending.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Material | $n$ (about) |
|---|---|
| air | 1.00 |
| water | 1.33 |
| glass | 1.5 |
| diamond | 2.42 |

</div>

For a ray that goes from air into the material:

$$
n = \frac{\sin i}{\sin r}
$$

- $i$: angle of incidence (in air)
- $r$: angle of refraction (in the material)
- Your calculator must be in **degrees** (D or DEG on the screen).

<WorkedExample
  question="A ray goes from air into a plastic block. The angle of incidence is 50° and the angle of refraction is 31°. Calculate the refractive index of the plastic."
  given={['$i = 50^\\circ$', '$r = 31^\\circ$']}
  find="the refractive index $n$"
  answer="$n = 1.49$"
>

$n = \dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r} = \dfrac{\sin 50^\circ}{\sin 31^\circ}$

$n = \dfrac{0.766}{0.515}$

$n = 1.49$ (no unit)

</WorkedExample>

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Extended only** (3.2.2 S6, S7). Core students: revise lesson 10.3 with the simulation, and do the reflection practice.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 14 min, measurement 14 min, practice 22 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Values:** the refractive indices in the table are typical values (water 1.33, crown glass about 1.5, diamond about 2.42). The syllabus defines $n$ as the ratio of the speeds of a wave in two regions.

**Common mistakes:**

- Calculator in radians.
- Dividing the angles instead of their sines.
- Giving $n$ a unit.
- $i$ and $r$ swapped when the light goes from glass into air: $i$ in the equation is always the angle **in air**.

**Language support:**

- Read $\dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r}$ as "sine i over sine r".
- *index* here means "a number that measures something".

**Differentiation:**

- Support: level 1 only, with the calculator steps written on the board.
- Stretch: level 3 (critical angle, speed of light in a material).

</Step>
