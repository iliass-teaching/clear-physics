<Step kind="learn" title="Drawing a ray diagram" minutes={15}>

To find the image of an object (an arrow standing on the principal axis), draw **two** (or three) rays from the **top** of the object:

1. A ray **parallel to the principal axis**. After the lens, it goes **through F** (on the other side).
2. A ray through the **centre of the lens**. It goes **straight on**.
3. (A check) A ray **through F** on the object side. After the lens, it goes **parallel** to the principal axis.

Where the rays **meet** is the top of the image.

<Figure svg={lensRealImageSvg()} caption="The object is further away than 2F: the image is real, inverted and diminished." width="36rem" />

**Describing an image:** use three words.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Question | Words |
|---|---|
| Is it bigger or smaller? | **enlarged** / **same size** / **diminished** |
| Which way up? | **upright** / **inverted** (upside down) |
| Do the rays really meet? | **real** (you can catch it on a screen) / **virtual** |

</div>

A **virtual image** forms where rays that spread out (diverge) **seem** to come from, when we follow them **backwards** (dashed lines). It does **not** appear on a screen.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 15 min, simulation (or practical) 20 min, check 15 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Syllabus:** ray diagrams for a **real** image are Core (3.2.3 C3). Ray diagrams for a **virtual** image with a converging lens are Extended (next lesson). The meaning of a virtual image (C5) is Core.

**Common mistakes:**

- Drawing rays from the bottom of the object.
- Drawing the ray through F on the wrong side.
- Not using a ruler; rays that miss each other.
- Writing "big" or "upside down": teach the exam words *enlarged* and *inverted*.

**Language support:**

- Three questions → three words: *size? way up? real?*
- *diminished* = made smaller.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: give a printed diagram with the lens, F and 2F, and the object already drawn.
- Stretch: what happens to the image when the object moves closer and closer to F?

</Step>
