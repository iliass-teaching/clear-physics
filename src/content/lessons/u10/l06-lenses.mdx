<Step kind="learn" title="Two types of lens" minutes={14}>

A lens is a curved piece of glass or plastic. Light **refracts** at both surfaces.

<Figure svg={lensesSvg()} caption="A parallel beam of light through a converging lens and a diverging lens." width="36rem" />

**Converging lens** (convex: thicker in the middle):

- Rays parallel to the principal axis **come together** (converge) at one point: the **principal focus** F (or **focal point**).
- Used in magnifying glasses, cameras, projectors, glasses for long sight.

**Diverging lens** (concave: thinner in the middle):

- Rays parallel to the principal axis **spread out** (diverge). They seem to come from a point F behind the lens.
- Used in glasses for short sight, and in some door viewers.

**Words:**

- **Principal axis:** the line through the centre of the lens, at 90° to the lens.
- **Principal focus (focal point) F:** where parallel rays meet (converging lens), or seem to come from (diverging lens). There is one F on each side of the lens.
- **Focal length $f$:** the distance from the centre of the lens to F.

A **fatter** (more curved) lens bends the light more: its focal length is **shorter**.

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Measure the focal length" minutes={18}>

**Equipment:** a converging lens in a holder, a white screen (card), a metre rule. You need a window or a lamp far away.

1. Point the lens at a window (or a distant lamp) on the other side of the room. Light from a far object arrives almost parallel.
2. Move the screen behind the lens until you see a **sharp** image of the window.
3. Measure the distance from the centre of the lens to the screen. This is the focal length.
4. Repeat 3 times and calculate the mean. Do the same with a fatter lens.

**Questions:**

1. Why must the object be far away?
2. Which lens has the shorter focal length? Is it the fatter or the thinner one?
3. Try with a diverging lens. Can you find a sharp image on the screen?

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 14 min, experiment 18 min, check 18 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Drawing convention:** in ray diagrams we draw a thin lens as a line (or a thin shape), and we bend each ray once, at the centre line of the lens, not at each surface.

**Common mistakes:**

- Confusing *converging* and *diverging*.
- Thinking a lens has only one focal point.
- Measuring the focal length from the edge of the lens.

**Language support:**

- *converge* = come together (like roads that meet); *diverge* = go apart.
- *convex* (bulges out) and *concave* (curves in, like a cave) are also used.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: give the outline of each lens and the parallel rays; students complete the rays.
- Stretch: why is the image of the window upside down on the screen?

**Safety:** never look at the Sun through a lens, and never focus sunlight on skin or paper.

</Step>
