<Step kind="learn" title="The law of reflection" minutes={12}>

<Figure svg={reflectionSvg()} caption="Reflection at a plane mirror." width="30rem" />

- The **normal** is a line at **90°** to the mirror, at the point where the ray hits it. We draw it as a dashed line.
- The **angle of incidence** $i$ is between the **incident ray** and the **normal**.
- The **angle of reflection** $r$ is between the **reflected ray** and the **normal**.

**Law of reflection:**

$$
\text{angle of incidence} = \text{angle of reflection} \qquad i = r
$$

**Important:** always measure the angles from the **normal**, not from the mirror.

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Test the law" minutes={18}>

**Equipment:** a ray box with a single slit, a plane mirror in a holder, a protractor, a sheet of white paper, a ruler.

1. Draw a line on the paper. Stand the mirror on it. Draw the normal at the middle.
2. Shine a ray at the point where the normal meets the mirror, at an angle of incidence of 20°.
3. Mark the reflected ray with two small crosses. Draw it with the ruler.
4. Measure the angle of reflection. Repeat for 30°, 40°, 50°, 60°, 70°.

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 12 min, experiment 18 min, practice 15 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Practical tips:** the reflecting surface of a glass mirror is at the back; draw the line along the back of the mirror. A darkened room helps.

**Common mistakes:**

- Measuring the angles from the mirror instead of the normal.
- Drawing rays without arrows, or with arrows in the wrong direction.
- Forgetting the normal.

**Language support:**

- *incident* = arriving; *reflected* = sent back.
- The letters: $i$ for **i**ncidence, $r$ for **r**eflection (and later, **r**efraction).

**Differentiation:**

- Support: give the drawing with the normal already drawn.
- Stretch: two mirrors are at 90° to each other. A ray hits the first mirror. Show that the final ray is parallel to the first ray.

**Safety:** ray boxes get hot. Do not touch the lamp.

</Step>
