<Step kind="learn" title="Permanent magnets and electromagnets" minutes={14}>

An **electromagnet** is a coil of wire with an electric current in it, usually around a **soft iron** core. When the current is **on**, it is a magnet. When the current is **off**, the soft iron loses its magnetism. (You will build one in Unit 15.)

<div class="table-wrap">

| | Permanent magnet | Electromagnet |
|---|---|---|
| Can you switch it off? | no | **yes** |
| Can you change its strength? | no | **yes** (change the current) |
| Needs electricity? | no | yes |
| Uses | fridge doors; compasses; loudspeakers and microphones; electric motors and generators; magnetic catches | scrapyard cranes (pick up and drop cars); relays and circuit breakers; electric bells; MRI scanners in hospitals |

</div>

**Choose:** use an electromagnet when you need to **switch** the magnet on and off, or change its strength. Use a permanent magnet when you need a magnet **all the time** without using energy.

</Step>
