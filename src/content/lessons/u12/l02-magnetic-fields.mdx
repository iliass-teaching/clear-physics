<Step kind="learn" title="The magnetic field" minutes={14}>

A **magnetic field** is a **region** around a magnet where a **magnetic pole feels a force**.

We draw the field with **field lines**:

<Figure svg={magnetFieldSvg('one', 'Magnetic field lines around a bar magnet, from N to S')} caption="The magnetic field of a bar magnet." width="34rem" />

- Outside the magnet, field lines go **from N to S**.
- The **direction** of the field at a point is the direction of the **force on a N pole** at that point. A compass needle's north end points along the field.
- Field lines **never cross**.
- **Extended:** where the lines are **closer together**, the field is **stronger** (near the poles).

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Plot the field" minutes={18}>

**With a compass:**

1. Put a bar magnet on a sheet of paper. Draw around it.
2. Put a small plotting compass near the N pole. Mark a dot at the end the needle points to.
3. Move the compass so its other end is on the dot. Mark the next dot. Repeat until you reach the S pole.
4. Join the dots: this is one field line. Add an arrow (N → S). Repeat from other starting points.

**With iron filings:** put a sheet of paper on the magnet. Sprinkle iron filings and tap the paper gently. The filings line up along the field lines. (They show the **pattern**, but not the **direction**.)

**In the simulation:** drag the compass around the magnet. Then tick "show many compasses" and "show the field lines".

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Two magnets (Extended)" minutes={8}>

<div class="graph-grid">
<Figure svg={magnetFieldSvg('attract', 'Field lines between a N pole and a S pole: they go across the gap')} caption="N facing S: the lines join the two magnets (attraction)." />
<Figure svg={magnetFieldSvg('repel', 'Field lines between two N poles: they push away from each other, with a neutral point in the middle')} caption="N facing N: the lines push apart (repulsion)." />
</div>

**Magnetic forces are caused by the interaction between magnetic fields.** Between two N poles, the fields push against each other: there is a **neutral point** in the middle, where the field is zero.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** 4.1 C5–C8 are Core. Forces as interactions between fields (S10) and spacing of lines (S11) are Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 14 min, plotting and simulation 18 min, two magnets 8 min, check 10 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**About the simulation:** the field is computed from a simple model (each pole acts like a point source). The patterns are realistic outside the magnets. Inside a real magnet, the field goes from S to N; the model does not show the inside.

**Common mistakes:**

- Arrows from S to N.
- Field lines that cross or that start in the air.
- "The compass needle points to the N pole of the magnet."

**Language support:**

- *field* here means "a region where a force acts".
- *plot* = mark points and join them.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: a printed magnet outline with the compass positions marked.
- Stretch: the Earth behaves like a big bar magnet. A compass N pole points to the Earth's geographic north. So what kind of magnetic pole is near the geographic North Pole? (A magnetic S pole.)

**Safety:** iron filings: do not touch the eyes; put a sheet of paper or plastic between the filings and the magnet.

</Step>
