<Step kind="learn" title="Poles and forces" minutes={12}>

Every magnet has two **poles**: a **north pole (N)** and a **south pole (S)**. The magnetic force is strongest at the poles.

<Figure svg={polesForcesSvg()} caption="Forces between the poles of two magnets." width="30rem" />

- **Unlike** poles (N and S) **attract**.
- **Like** poles (N and N, or S and S) **repel**.

**Is it a magnet?** A magnet attracts **both** ends of an unmagnetised piece of iron. Only **repulsion** proves that an object is a magnet: two magnets can repel, but a magnet never repels an unmagnetised piece of iron.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Magnetic materials and induced magnetism" minutes={12}>

**Magnetic materials** are attracted by a magnet: **iron**, **steel** (mostly iron), **nickel** and **cobalt**. They can be **magnetised** (made into magnets).

**Non-magnetic materials** are not attracted: for example copper, aluminium, gold, plastic, wood, glass.

**Induced magnetism:** when a magnetic material is near a magnet, it **becomes a magnet** too.

<Figure svg={inducedSvg()} caption="A magnet induces magnetism in an iron nail." width="30rem" />

**Temporary and permanent magnets:**

<div class="table-wrap">

| | Soft iron | Steel |
|---|---|---|
| Magnetise it | easy | harder |
| Remove the magnet | it **loses** its magnetism quickly | it **keeps** its magnetism |
| Type of magnet | **temporary** | **permanent** |
| Used for | the core of electromagnets | permanent magnets (fridge magnets, compasses) |

</div>

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Iron or steel?" minutes={14}>

**Equipment:** a bar magnet, a soft iron nail, a steel nail (or a steel needle) of the same size, paper clips.

1. Hold the **iron** nail against one pole of the magnet. How many paper clips can the nail hold?
2. Pull the magnet away. What happens to the paper clips?
3. Repeat with the **steel** nail.
4. Stroke the steel needle 20 times with one pole of the magnet, always in the same direction. Can it now pick up a paper clip? Hang it from a thread: which way does it point?

**Write:** which one is a temporary magnet? Which one is a permanent magnet? Use your results.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 24 min, activity 14 min, check 12 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Note:** "soft" iron does not mean physically soft: it means it is magnetically soft (easy to magnetise and demagnetise).

**Common mistakes:**

- "All metals are magnetic."
- "Attraction proves an object is a magnet."
- "A magnet only attracts the opposite pole of an iron bar." (It attracts both ends, by induced magnetism.)

**Language support:**

- *like poles* = the same poles; *unlike poles* = different poles.
- *induce* = cause something to happen without touching (or by being near).

**Differentiation:**

- Support: a sorting grid (magnetic / non-magnetic) with real objects.
- Stretch: the homework puzzle.

**Safety:** keep strong magnets away from phones, bank cards and pacemakers. Neodymium magnets can pinch fingers.

</Step>
