<Step kind="learn" title="Hazards" minutes={14}>

The mains supply is **alternating current** at a high p.d. (about 230 V in many countries; 220 V in Thailand). It can **kill**.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Hazard | Why it is dangerous |
|---|---|
| **damaged insulation** (cracked or cut plastic on a cable) | someone can touch the metal wire inside and get an electric shock; it can also cause a short circuit and a fire |
| **overheating cables** | a big current heats the wire; the insulation can melt and start a **fire** |
| **damp conditions** (wet hands, water near sockets) | water conducts electricity: a current can flow through a person and give a shock |
| **overloading** plugs, extension leads and sockets (too many appliances) | the total current is too big: the cables overheat and can start a fire |

</div>

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Three wires" minutes={12}>

<Figure svg={mainsCircuitSvg()} caption="A mains circuit with an appliance that has a metal case." width="34rem" />

- **Live wire** (also called the **line** wire): it carries the high alternating p.d. In many countries it is **brown**.
- **Neutral wire**: it completes the circuit; it is at (or close to) 0 V. Often **blue**.
- **Earth wire**: a safety wire, connected to the metal case and to the ground. Often **green and yellow**.

**Why is the switch in the live wire?** When the switch is **off**, it disconnects the appliance from the **high p.d.** If the switch were in the neutral wire, the appliance would still be connected to the live wire when "off": touching a part inside it could still give a shock.

The **fuse** is also in the live wire, for the same reason (next lesson).

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core:** 4.4 C1, C2.

**Timing:** starter 8 min, hazards 14 min, three wires 12 min, check 18 min, exit ticket 8 min.

**Local context:** mains p.d. and plug types differ between countries (for example 230 V in the UK and much of Europe, 220 V in Thailand, 120 V in the USA). Wire colours also differ in older installations. Teach the principles; use the local colours and plugs as examples.

**Common mistakes:**

- "The earth wire carries the current back." (That is the neutral wire; the earth wire carries current only in a fault.)
- "The switch can be in any wire."

**Language support:**

- *hazard* = something that can cause harm.
- *insulation* = the plastic around a wire.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: a picture of a room with 6 hazards to find and explain.
- Stretch: why is it dangerous to use a hair dryer in a bathroom, even if it is switched off at the appliance?

**Safety:** never open a mains plug or appliance in class. Use pictures or a sealed demonstration plug only.

</Step>
