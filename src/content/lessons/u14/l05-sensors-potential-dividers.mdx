<Step kind="starter" title="Automatic lights" minutes={5}>

A street light switches on by itself when it gets dark. Which component from lesson 14.1 could "sense" the dark? How could a change in resistance switch something on?

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Bigger resistance, bigger p.d." minutes={10}>

In a series circuit, the **same current** flows through every component. Since $V = IR$:

- the component with the **bigger resistance** has the **bigger p.d.** across it.

<Figure svg={potentialDividerSvg()} caption="A potential divider: two resistors share the supply p.d." width="24rem" />

Two resistors in series **divide** (share) the supply p.d. This is a **potential divider**.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="The potential divider equation (Extended)" minutes={12}>

The p.d.s are in the **same ratio** as the resistances:

$$
\frac{R_1}{R_2} = \frac{V_1}{V_2}
$$

and $V_1 + V_2 = V$ (the supply p.d.).

<WorkedExample
  question="A 9 V supply is connected across a 2 kΩ resistor and a 1 kΩ resistor in series. Calculate the p.d. across each resistor."
  given={['$V = 9\\,\\text{V}$', '$R_1 = 2\\,\\text{k}\\Omega$', '$R_2 = 1\\,\\text{k}\\Omega$']}
  find="$V_1$ and $V_2$"
  answer="$V_1 = 6\,\text{V}$; $V_2 = 3\,\text{V}$"
>

$\dfrac{V_1}{V_2} = \dfrac{R_1}{R_2} = \dfrac{2}{1}$: $V_1$ is twice $V_2$.

The 9 V is shared in the ratio 2 : 1 (3 parts): $V_1 = 6\,\text{V}$ and $V_2 = 3\,\text{V}$.

</WorkedExample>

**A variable potential divider:** if one resistor is a variable resistor, a thermistor or an LDR, the output p.d. **changes**:

- LDR at the bottom: in the **dark**, its resistance is **high**, so the p.d. across it is **high**. This can switch on a street light.
- Thermistor at the top: when it is **hot**, its resistance is **low**, so the p.d. across the bottom resistor is **high**. This can switch on a fire alarm.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** 4.3.3 C1 is Core. The variable potential divider (S2) and the ratio equation (S3) are Extended. Core students can use the simulation to see how sensors change the output.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 22 min, simulation 15 min, practice 13 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**About the simulation:** the LDR goes from about 100 kΩ (dark) to 1 kΩ (bright light). The thermistor is an NTC thermistor of 10 kΩ at 25 °C. These are typical values for school sensors. The output device switches on above 3.0 V.

**Common mistakes:**

- Giving the bigger p.d. to the smaller resistor.
- Forgetting that the output is taken across one particular resistor (usually the bottom one).

**Language support:**

- *divide* = share.
- *ratio* 2 : 1 = two parts and one part.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: the worked example with resistors of equal value first.
- Stretch: design a circuit that switches on a heater when it gets cold.

</Step>
