<Step kind="learn" title="Rules for series circuits" minutes={14}>

<Figure svg={seriesParallelSvg()} caption="A series circuit (left) has only one path for the current." width="34rem" />

In a **series circuit**, all the components are in **one loop**:

1. The **current is the same** at every point: $I_1 = I_2 = I_3$.
2. If one component breaks, the circuit is **broken**: everything stops.
3. **Cells in series:** their e.m.f.s **add up** (if they point the same way). Two 1.5 V cells give 3.0 V.
4. **Resistors in series:** their resistances **add up**: $R = R_1 + R_2 + \dots$
5. **Extended:** the **p.d.s** across the components **add up** to the total p.d. of the supply: $V = V_1 + V_2 + \dots$

<WorkedExample
  question="A 4 Ω resistor and an 8 Ω resistor are in series with a 6 V battery. Find (a) the combined resistance, (b) the current, (c) the p.d. across the 8 Ω resistor."
  given={['$R_1 = 4\\,\\Omega$', '$R_2 = 8\\,\\Omega$', '$E = 6\\,\\text{V}$']}
  find="$R$, $I$ and $V_2$"
  answer="$R = 12\,\Omega$; $I = 0.5\,\text{A}$; $V_2 = 4\,\text{V}$"
>

(a) $R = 4 + 8 = 12\,\Omega$

(b) $I = \dfrac{V}{R} = \dfrac{6}{12} = 0.5\,\text{A}$

(c) $V_2 = I R_2 = 0.5 \times 8 = 4\,\text{V}$ (and $V_1 = 2\,\text{V}$: $2 + 4 = 6\,\text{V}$ ✓)

</WorkedExample>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** 4.3.2 C1–C4 are Core. Adding p.d.s in series (S8b) is Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 14 min, simulation 14 min, practice 20 min, exit ticket 7 min.

**About the simulation:** the lamps have a constant resistance, so the numbers are simple. Real filament lamps change resistance (Unit 13).

**Common mistakes:**

- "The current is used up by the first lamp."
- Adding currents in a series circuit.
- Cells in series the wrong way round (their e.m.f.s subtract).

**Language support:**

- *in series* = one after the other, in one line.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: level 1 and 2 of the practice.
- Stretch: a 9 V battery is in series with a lamp and a resistor. The p.d. across the lamp is 6 V and the current is 0.2 A. Find the resistance of the resistor.

</Step>
