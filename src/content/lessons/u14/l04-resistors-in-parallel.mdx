<Step kind="starter" title="Smaller than both" minutes={5}>

In the simulation of the last lesson, two 6 Ω lamps in parallel had a combined resistance of 3 Ω. How can two resistors together have **less** resistance than one of them alone?

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="The equation (Extended)" minutes={14}>

For two resistors $R_1$ and $R_2$ in parallel, the combined resistance $R$ is given by:

$$
\frac{1}{R} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2}
$$

You can also use: $R = \dfrac{R_1 R_2}{R_1 + R_2}$ ("product over sum").

**Check your answer:** $R$ must be **smaller** than the smaller resistor.

<WorkedExample
  question="A 6 Ω resistor and a 3 Ω resistor are in parallel. Calculate the combined resistance."
  given={['$R_1 = 6\\,\\Omega$', '$R_2 = 3\\,\\Omega$']}
  find="the combined resistance $R$"
  answer="$R = 2\,\Omega$"
>

$\dfrac{1}{R} = \dfrac{1}{6} + \dfrac{1}{3} = \dfrac{1}{6} + \dfrac{2}{6} = \dfrac{3}{6}$

$R = \dfrac{6}{3} = 2\,\Omega$ (smaller than 3 Ω ✓)

</WorkedExample>

**Two equal resistors** in parallel: the combined resistance is **half** of one resistor.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Extended only** (4.3.2 S10). Core students can repeat the series and parallel investigations with different lamps, and do levels 1 of the series and parallel practice.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 14 min, simulation 12 min, practice 22 min, exit ticket 7 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- Forgetting to take the reciprocal at the end.
- Adding resistances in parallel.

**Language support:**

- *reciprocal* = 1 divided by the number.
- "product over sum": multiply them, then divide by their sum.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: start with pairs of equal resistors.
- Stretch: a 6 Ω resistor is in series with two 6 Ω resistors in parallel. Find the total resistance (9 Ω).
