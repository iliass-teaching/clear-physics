<Step kind="learn" title="The symbols" minutes={20}>

Circuit diagrams use **standard symbols**, so that other people can read them. Draw the wires as **straight lines**, with a ruler, and with **no gaps**.

<SymbolTable />

**Two sensors:**

- **Thermistor (NTC):** when the **temperature goes up**, its resistance goes **down**. Used in fire alarms and thermostats.
- **LDR:** when **more light** falls on it, its resistance goes **down**. Used in automatic street lights and cameras.

**A relay** is a switch worked by an electromagnet. A small current in the coil can switch on a big current in another circuit (for example a car starter motor).

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Read and build" minutes={18}>

1. Draw circuit diagrams for:
   - (a) a cell, a closed switch and a lamp;
   - (b) a battery, a variable resistor and a motor, with an ammeter to measure the current;
   - (c) a power supply, a fixed resistor and an LDR in series, with a voltmeter across the LDR.
2. Build circuit (b). What happens to the motor when you change the variable resistor?
3. **Extended:** add a diode in series with a lamp. Turn the diode round. What happens? Why?

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** all the symbols in 4.3.1 C1 are Core. Diodes and LEDs (S2) are Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, symbols 20 min, drawing and building 18 min, check 12 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Note on symbols:** symbols vary a little between countries and books. The ones here follow the usual IGCSE style (a lamp as a circle with a cross, a resistor as a rectangle).

**Common mistakes:**

- Gaps in wires, or wires that do not join at the corners.
- Drawing a voltmeter in series.
- Confusing the LDR and thermistor symbols (look for the arrows of light).

**Language support:**

- *component* = a part of a circuit.
- *sensor* = a component that responds to something (light, temperature).

**Differentiation:**

- Support: a matching game with symbol cards and name cards.
- Stretch: draw a circuit where a thermistor and a relay switch on a fan when it gets hot.

</Step>
