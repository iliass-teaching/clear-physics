<Step kind="learn" title="Half-life" minutes={12}>

We cannot predict when **one** nucleus decays. But in a big sample, a fixed **fraction** decays in a fixed time.

**The half-life of an isotope is the time taken for half the nuclei of that isotope in any sample to decay.**

After 1 half-life: ½ left. After 2 half-lives: ¼ left. After 3 half-lives: ⅛ left.

  caption="A decay curve. The count rate halves every 4 days: the half-life is 4 days."
/>

**From a decay curve:** read the start value, find the time when it is half of this, and the time when it is a quarter. The time between halvings is the half-life.

<WorkedExample
  question="Iodine-131 has a half-life of 8 days. A sample has a count rate of 1200 counts/minute. What is the count rate after 24 days?"
  given={['half-life $= 8$ days', 'start: 1200 counts/minute', 'time $= 24$ days']}
  find="the count rate after 24 days"
  answer="150 counts/minute"
>

Number of half-lives $= \dfrac{24}{8} = 3$

1200 → 600 → 300 → 150 counts/minute

</WorkedExample>

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** 5.2.4 C1 is Core (calculations without background). Data with background (S2) is Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 12 min, simulation 16 min, practice 20 min, exit ticket 7 min.

**About the simulation:** each nucleus has the same probability of decaying each minute. Isotope A has a half-life of 2 minutes, B of 4 minutes, C of 6 minutes (do not tell students before they measure it).

**Common mistakes:**

- Dividing by the number of half-lives instead of halving repeatedly.
- Reading the half-life from a curve that includes background (Extended: subtract it first).
- "After two half-lives, everything has decayed."

**Language support:**

- *half-life* = the time to go down to half.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: the coin activity with a table to complete.
- Stretch: why can we never say exactly when the last nucleus will decay?

</Step>
