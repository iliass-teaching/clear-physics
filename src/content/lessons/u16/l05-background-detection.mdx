<Step kind="learn" title="Background radiation" minutes={12}>

**Background radiation** is the ionising radiation that is around us **all the time**. Most of it is natural.

Main sources:

- **radon gas** in the air (from rocks under the ground);
- **rocks and buildings** (for example granite);
- **food and drink** (they contain small amounts of radioactive isotopes, like potassium-40);
- **cosmic rays** (radiation from space).

Smaller sources: medical uses (X-rays, scans) and the nuclear industry.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Detecting radiation" minutes={12}>

<Figure svg={detectorSvg()} caption="A detector connected to a counter." width="30rem" />

- Ionising radiation is measured with a **detector** (for example a Geiger–Müller tube) connected to a **counter**.
- Each time radiation enters the detector, the counter adds 1 (a "count").
- The **count rate** is the number of counts each second (**counts/s**) or each minute (**counts/minute**):

$$
\text{count rate} = \frac{\text{number of counts}}{\text{time}}
$$

**Extended: corrected count rate.** The detector also counts the background. To find the count rate from a source only:

$$
\text{corrected count rate} = \text{measured count rate} - \text{background count rate}
$$

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Measure the background" minutes={12}>

**With a detector (if your school has one):**

1. Remove all sources from the room. Count for 5 minutes. Calculate the background count rate in counts/minute.
2. Repeat 3 times. Are the results exactly the same? Why not? (Radioactive decay is random.)
3. Calculate the mean background count rate.

**No detector?** Use these results from a school: 103, 95, 118, 99, 110 counts in 5 minutes each. Calculate the mean count rate in counts/minute and in counts/s.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** 5.2.1 C1–C4 are Core. The corrected count rate (S5) is Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, background 12 min, detection 12 min, measurement 12 min, practice 14 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Activity data:** the mean of 103, 95, 118, 99, 110 is 105 counts in 5 minutes: 21 counts/minute, or 0.35 counts/s.

**Common mistakes:**

- "Background radiation comes only from nuclear power stations."
- Forgetting to subtract the background (Extended).
- Mixing counts/s and counts/minute.

**Language support:**

- *background* = always there, behind everything else.
- *cosmic* = from space.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: level 1 of the practice.
- Stretch: why is the background higher in some regions (for example on granite rock)?

**Safety:** follow your school's rules for radioactive sources (see lesson 16.9).
