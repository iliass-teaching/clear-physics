<Step kind="starter" title="Energy from nuclei" minutes={5}>

A nuclear power station and the Sun both get their energy from nuclei. But they do opposite things to the nuclei. What do you think each one does?

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Fission and fusion (Extended)" minutes={18}>

<Figure svg={fissionFusionSvg()} caption="Nuclear fission and nuclear fusion." width="38rem" />

**Fission: a big nucleus splits.**

A slow **neutron** hits a big nucleus, for example uranium-235. The nucleus **splits** into two smaller nuclei, and gives out 2 or 3 neutrons and a lot of **energy**. For example:

$$
{}^{235}_{92}\text{U} + {}^{1}_{0}\text{n} \rightarrow {}^{141}_{56}\text{Ba} + {}^{92}_{36}\text{Kr} + 3\,{}^{1}_{0}\text{n} + \text{energy}
$$

The new neutrons can split more nuclei: a **chain reaction**. Nuclear power stations use fission.

**Fusion: small nuclei join.**

Two small nuclei **join** to make a bigger nucleus, and give out a lot of **energy**. For example, two isotopes of hydrogen:

$$
{}^{2}_{1}\text{H} + {}^{3}_{1}\text{H} \rightarrow {}^{4}_{2}\text{He} + {}^{1}_{0}\text{n} + \text{energy}
$$

The Sun and other stars get their energy from fusion (Unit 17). Fusion needs very high temperatures and pressures.

**Mass and energy:** in both fission and fusion, the total **mass** of the products is a little **smaller** than the mass at the start. This "lost" mass has become **energy**.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Checking nuclide equations" minutes={10}>

In a nuclide equation, the numbers must **balance**:

- the **top numbers** ($A$) on the left add up to the top numbers on the right;
- the **bottom numbers** ($Z$) on the left add up to the bottom numbers on the right.

Check the fission equation: top: $235 + 1 = 141 + 92 + 3 \times 1 = 236$ ✓. Bottom: $92 + 0 = 56 + 36 + 0 = 92$ ✓.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Extended only** (5.1.2 S6). Core students can review lesson 16.3 and do the nuclide practice.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, fission and fusion 18 min, equations 10 min, check 20 min, exit ticket 7 min.

**Syllabus note:** mass and energy changes are qualitative only ("without values"). $E = mc^2$ is not required.

**Common mistakes:**

- Mixing up fission (splitting) and fusion (joining).
- Forgetting the neutrons when balancing.

**Language support:**

- *fission* (like "fissure", a crack: splitting); *fusion* (like "fuse", join).

**Differentiation:**

- Support: two picture cards to label.
- Stretch: why is fusion so difficult to use on Earth?

</Step>
