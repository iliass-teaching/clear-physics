<Step kind="learn" title="Particles and numbers" minutes={15}>

<div class="table-wrap">

| Particle | Where? | Relative charge | Relative mass |
|---|---|---|---|
| proton | nucleus | +1 | 1 |
| neutron | nucleus | 0 | 1 |
| electron | orbits | −1 | very small (about 1/2000) |

</div>

Protons and neutrons are called **nucleons**.

- **Proton number** (atomic number) $Z$: the number of **protons**. It tells you the **element**.
- **Nucleon number** (mass number) $A$: the number of **protons + neutrons**.
- **Number of neutrons** $= A - Z$.

<Figure svg={nuclideNotationSvg()} caption="Nuclide notation." width="28rem" />

**Nuclide notation:** ${}^{A}_{Z}\text{X}$, where X is the symbol of the element.

**Extended:** the **relative charge** of a nucleus is $+Z$ (its protons); its **relative mass** is about $A$ (its nucleons).

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Isotopes" minutes={10}>

<Figure svg={isotopesSvg()} caption="Two isotopes of carbon." width="28rem" />

**Isotopes** are atoms of the **same element** (same number of protons) with **different numbers of neutrons**. An element can have several isotopes.

- Carbon-12: ${}^{12}_{6}\text{C}$, 6 neutrons. Carbon-14: ${}^{14}_{6}\text{C}$, 8 neutrons.
- Isotopes have the same chemistry (same electrons), but different masses. Some isotopes are radioactive.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** 5.1.2 C1–C5 are Core. The relative charge and mass of a nucleus (S7, S8) are Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, numbers 15 min, isotopes 10 min, practice 22 min, exit ticket 8 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- Confusing $A$ and $Z$ (the bigger number is $A$, at the top).
- "Isotopes have different numbers of protons."
- Adding instead of subtracting for the number of neutrons.

**Language support:**

- *nucleon* = a particle in the nucleus (proton or neutron).
- *isotope*: "iso" = same, "tope" = place (the same place in the periodic table).

**Differentiation:**

- Support: a table to complete for 5 nuclides.
- Stretch: hydrogen has three isotopes. Write them in nuclide notation.
