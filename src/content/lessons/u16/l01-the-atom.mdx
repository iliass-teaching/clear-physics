<Step kind="starter" title="How small?" minutes={5}>

A single atom is about 0.000 000 000 1 m across ($10^{-10}$ m). How many atoms would fit across the width of a hair (about 0.1 mm)? What do you think is inside an atom?

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Inside an atom" minutes={15}>

<Figure svg={atomSvg()} caption="The nuclear model of the atom (not to scale)." width="34rem" />

- In the centre is a very small **nucleus**. It has a **positive** charge.
- The nucleus contains **protons** (positive) and **neutrons** (no charge).
- **Electrons** (negative) **orbit** the nucleus.
- An atom has the same number of protons and electrons, so it is **neutral** (no overall charge).
- The nucleus is about **100 000 times smaller** than the atom: an atom is mostly **empty space**. But the nucleus has almost all the mass.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Ions" minutes={12}>

<Figure svg={ionsSvg()} caption="Atoms can lose or gain electrons." width="34rem" />

An **ion** is an atom that has lost or gained electrons. (The nucleus does not change.)

- An atom that **loses** electrons becomes a **positive ion** (more protons than electrons).
- An atom that **gains** electrons becomes a **negative ion** (more electrons than protons).

Example: a sodium atom has 11 protons and 11 electrons. It loses 1 electron: it becomes a positive ion, Na⁺, with 11 protons and 10 electrons.

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Model an atom" minutes={13}>

Make a scale model of an atom in the school field (or imagine it): if the nucleus were a pea (about 5 mm) on the centre spot of a football pitch, where would the electrons be? (If the atom is 100 000 times bigger than the nucleus, the atom is about 500 m across: the electrons are outside the stadium.)

Then, with small sticky dots of three colours, build these on paper:

1. A helium atom: 2 protons, 2 neutrons, 2 electrons.
2. A lithium atom: 3 protons, 4 neutrons, 3 electrons.
3. A lithium ion Li⁺. What did you change?

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core:** 5.1.1 C1, C2.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, atom 15 min, ions 12 min, model 13 min, check 10 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Numbers:** an atom is about $10^{-10}$ m across, a nucleus about $10^{-15}$ to $10^{-14}$ m: the nucleus is roughly 10 000 to 100 000 times smaller.

**Common mistakes:**

- "Ions form when atoms gain or lose protons."
- "The atom is like a small solid ball."
- Confusing *neutron* and *neutral*.

**Language support:**

- *nucleus* (plural *nuclei*) = the centre.
- *orbit* = go round and round.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: a labelled diagram with blanks.
- Stretch: why is the word "orbit" only a simple model for electrons? (Modern physics describes them as clouds of probability; not in the syllabus.)

</Step>
