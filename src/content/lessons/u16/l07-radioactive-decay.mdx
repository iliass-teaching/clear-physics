<Step kind="starter" title="A different element" minutes={5}>

Uranium-238 gives out an α-particle. Afterwards, the nucleus is no longer uranium. How can a nucleus change into a different element?

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Radioactive decay" minutes={12}>

**Radioactive decay** is a change in an **unstable** nucleus. It gives out α-particles, β-particles and/or γ-radiation.

- It is **spontaneous**: it happens by itself; temperature, pressure or chemistry do not change it.
- It is **random**: we cannot predict **when** one particular nucleus will decay.

During **α-decay** or **β-decay**, the number of protons changes, so the nucleus becomes a **different element**.

**γ-emission** does not change the number of protons or neutrons: the nucleus just loses some energy.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Why and how (Extended)" minutes={15}>

**Why are some isotopes radioactive?** Their nucleus has **too many neutrons**, or it is **too heavy** (too many nucleons).

**Each decay makes the nucleus more stable:**

<div class="table-wrap">

| Decay | What happens in the nucleus | $A$ | $Z$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **α** | loses 2 protons and 2 neutrons | − 4 | − 2 |
| **β** | a neutron changes into a proton and an electron; the electron leaves | no change | + 1 |
| **γ** | loses energy only | no change | no change |

</div>

β-decay: **neutron → proton + electron**. It reduces the number of excess neutrons.

**Decay equations** (nuclide notation). The top numbers and the bottom numbers balance:

$$
{}^{238}_{92}\text{U} \rightarrow {}^{234}_{90}\text{Th} + {}^{4}_{2}\alpha
\qquad
{}^{14}_{6}\text{C} \rightarrow {}^{14}_{7}\text{N} + {}^{0}_{-1}\beta
$$

(An α-particle can also be written ${}^{4}_{2}\text{He}$, and a β-particle ${}^{0}_{-1}\text{e}$.)

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** 5.2.3 C1, C2 are Core. Reasons for instability, effects on the nucleus, and decay equations (S3–S5) are Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, decay 12 min, Extended 15 min, practice 20 min, exit ticket 8 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- In β-decay, writing $Z - 1$ instead of $Z + 1$.
- In α-decay, subtracting 2 from $A$ instead of 4.
- "The electron in β-decay comes from the electron cloud." (It comes from the nucleus.)

**Language support:**

- *spontaneous* = by itself; *unstable* = likely to change.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: the table of changes on a card during the practice.
- Stretch: uranium-238 decays in many steps to lead-206. How many α-decays are needed? (8, because $238 - 206 = 32 = 8 \times 4$.)
