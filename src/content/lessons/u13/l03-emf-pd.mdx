<Step kind="learn" title="E.m.f. and p.d." minutes={14}>

<Figure svg={energyFlowSvg()} caption="The source gives energy to the charges; the components take energy from them." width="30rem" />

**Electromotive force (e.m.f.):** the electrical **work done by a source** (a cell, a battery, a power supply) in moving **one unit of charge** (1 C) around a **complete circuit**.

**Potential difference (p.d.):** the **work done by one unit of charge** (1 C) passing **through a component** (the energy it transfers to the component).

Both are measured in **volts** (V). 1 V = 1 J per coulomb.

- A 9 V battery gives 9 J of energy to each coulomb of charge.
- If the p.d. across a lamp is 6 V, each coulomb transfers 6 J of energy in the lamp.

**Extended: the equations**

$$
E = \frac{W}{Q} \qquad V = \frac{W}{Q}
$$

- $E$: e.m.f. (V); $V$: p.d. (V); $W$: work done, or energy transferred (J); $Q$: charge (C).

<WorkedExample
  question="A charge of 20 C passes through a heater. 240 J of energy is transferred. Calculate the p.d. across the heater."
  given={['$W = 240\\,\\text{J}$', '$Q = 20\\,\\text{C}$']}
  find="the p.d. $V$"
  answer="$V = 12\,\text{V}$"
>

$V = \dfrac{W}{Q} = \dfrac{240}{20}$

$V = 12\,\text{V}$

</WorkedExample>

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Using a voltmeter" minutes={8}>

<Figure svg={measureCircuitSvg()} caption="A voltmeter is connected in parallel (across the component)." width="32rem" />

- A **voltmeter** measures p.d. (or e.m.f.). It is connected **in parallel**: one lead on each side of the component.
- Like ammeters, voltmeters can be **analogue** (two scales, for example 0–3 V and 0–15 V) or **digital**. Choose the range: start high, then go lower for a more precise reading.
- To measure the **e.m.f.** of a cell, connect the voltmeter across the cell.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** the definitions and voltmeters (4.2.3 C1–C5) are Core. The equations (S6, S7) are Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, e.m.f. and p.d. 14 min, voltmeters 8 min, practical 10 min, practice 15 min, exit ticket 8 min.

**Model:** the rope model or a "ski lift" model: the cell lifts the charges (gives them energy); the components let them go down (take energy).

**Common mistakes:**

- "The voltage flows through the circuit." Current flows; p.d. is *across* a component.
- Connecting a voltmeter in series.
- Mixing up e.m.f. (the source gives energy) and p.d. (a component takes energy).

**Language support:**

- *across* (p.d., voltmeter) versus *through* (current, ammeter).
- *e.m.f.* is not a force, despite its name.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: the definitions with gaps to fill.
- Stretch: level 3 of the practice (two steps).
