<Step kind="learn" title="Using an ammeter" minutes={12}>

- An **ammeter** measures current. It is connected **in series**: the current must go **through** it.
- In a series circuit, the ammeter reads the same anywhere in the loop (Unit 14).

<Figure svg={measureCircuitSvg()} caption="An ammeter is in series. (The voltmeter, in parallel, is for the next lesson.)" width="32rem" />

**Analogue ammeters** have a needle and a scale. Many have two **ranges** (for example 0–1 A and 0–5 A).

- Read the scale that matches the range you connected.
- Look straight at the needle (not from the side) to avoid a parallax error.

**Digital ammeters** show a number. Choose a range: for a small current (for example 0.025 A), a **milliamp** range (mA) gives more digits: 25.0 mA. 1 mA = 0.001 A.

**Which range?** Start with the **highest** range (so the meter is not damaged), then change to a lower range to get a more precise reading.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Charge and current (Extended)" minutes={10}>

Current is the **charge** passing a point **each second**:

$$
I = \frac{Q}{t}
$$

- $I$: current (A); $Q$: charge (C, coulombs); $t$: time (s).
- So 1 A = 1 C/s (one coulomb each second).

<WorkedExample
  question="A current of 0.5 A flows through a lamp for 2 minutes. How much charge passes through the lamp?"
  given={['$I = 0.5\\,\\text{A}$', '$t = 2\\,\\text{min} = 120\\,\\text{s}$']}
  find="the charge $Q$"
  answer="$Q = 60\,\text{C}$"
>

$I = \dfrac{Q}{t}$, so $Q = I t$

$Q = 0.5 \times 120$

$Q = 60\,\text{C}$

</WorkedExample>

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** using ammeters (4.2.2 C2) is Core. $I = \dfrac{Q}{t}$ (S5) is Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, ammeters 12 min, charge and current 10 min, practice 20 min, exit ticket 8 min.

**Starter answer:** 0.62 A on the 0–1 A scale; 3.1 A on the 0–5 A scale.

**Common mistakes:**

- Reading the wrong scale on a two-range meter.
- Connecting an ammeter in parallel (it has a very small resistance: a big current can damage it).
- Time in minutes in $Q = It$.

**Language support:**

- *range* = the biggest value a meter can measure with one setting.
- *precise* = with more digits (smaller steps).

**Differentiation:**

- Support: several printed dials to read.
- Stretch: an ammeter has a resistance of 0.1 Ω. Why must it be very small?

</Step>
