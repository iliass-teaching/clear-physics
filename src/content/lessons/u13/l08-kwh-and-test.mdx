<Step kind="learn" title="The kilowatt-hour" minutes={10}>

The joule is a very small unit for the energy used in a home. Electricity companies use the **kilowatt-hour** (kW h):

**1 kW h is the energy transferred by a 1 kW appliance in 1 hour.**

$$
\text{energy (kW h)} = \text{power (kW)} \times \text{time (h)}
$$

$$
\text{cost} = \text{energy (kW h)} \times \text{price of 1 kW h}
$$

(1 kW h = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3 600 000 J.)

<WorkedExample
  question="A 2.5 kW air conditioner is used for 6 hours. One kW h costs 4 baht. Calculate the cost."
  given={['$P = 2.5\\,\\text{kW}$', '$t = 6\\,\\text{h}$', 'price $= 4$ baht per kW h']}
  find="the cost"
  answer="60 baht"
>

energy $= 2.5 \times 6 = 15\,\text{kW h}$

cost $= 15 \times 4 = 60$ baht

</WorkedExample>

**Units first:** W → kW: divide by 1000. Minutes → hours: divide by 60.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Timing:** kilowatt-hour 10 min, practice 17 min, unit test 33 min.

**Using the test:** questions 1–8 test Core content; 9–10 are Extended.

**Context:** the worked example uses Thai baht. The price is only an example: ask students to find the real price per kW h on an electricity bill at home.

**Common mistakes:**

- Using W instead of kW, or minutes instead of hours.
- Thinking the kilowatt-hour is a unit of power.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: a table "appliance / power in kW / hours / kW h / cost" to complete.
- Stretch: convert 1 kW h into joules, and explain why the kW h is more useful for bills.

</Step>
