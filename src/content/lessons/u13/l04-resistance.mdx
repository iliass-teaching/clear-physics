<Step kind="learn" title="What is resistance?" minutes={12}>

**Resistance** tells us how much a component **opposes** the current. With the same p.d., a bigger resistance gives a smaller current.

$$
R = \frac{V}{I}
$$

<div class="table-wrap">

| Quantity | Symbol | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| resistance | $R$ | ohm (Ω) |
| potential difference | $V$ | volt (V) |
| current | $I$ | ampere (A) |

</div>

1 Ω = 1 V/A. To find $V$ or $I$, change the subject: $V = IR$ and $I = \dfrac{V}{R}$.

<WorkedExample
  question="The p.d. across a lamp is 6.0 V. The current in it is 0.25 A. Calculate its resistance."
  given={['$V = 6.0\\,\\text{V}$', '$I = 0.25\\,\\text{A}$']}
  find="the resistance $R$"
  answer="$R = 24\,\Omega$"
>

$R = \dfrac{V}{I} = \dfrac{6.0}{0.25}$

$R = 24\,\Omega$

</WorkedExample>

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Measure a resistance" minutes={18}>

<Figure svg={measureCircuitSvg()} caption="The circuit to measure a resistance." width="32rem" />

**Method:**

1. Connect the circuit: the ammeter **in series**, the voltmeter **in parallel** with the resistor.
2. Use the variable resistor to set a small current. Record $V$ and $I$.
3. Change the variable resistor to get 5 or 6 different pairs of readings.
4. Calculate $R = \dfrac{V}{I}$ for each pair, then the **mean**.
5. Switch off between readings, so the resistor does not get hot.

**Virtual version:** choose the **fixed resistor**. Record at least 6 readings and calculate $R$ each time.

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 12 min, practical 18 min, practice 20 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Practical tips:** use a 10–50 Ω resistor and a supply of a few volts so the current stays small. Check the meter connections before switching on.

**Common mistakes:**

- $R = V \times I$.
- mA and kΩ not converted (level 3 of the practice).
- The voltmeter connected across the resistor and the ammeter together.

**Language support:**

- *resist* = to fight against, to oppose.
- Read Ω as "ohm" (the Greek letter omega).

**Differentiation:**

- Support: level 1 only, with $R = \dfrac{V}{I}$ on the board.
- Stretch: in the simulation, measure the filament lamp too. Is its resistance constant? (Next lessons.)

**Safety:** resistors and lamps can get hot. Low voltages only.

</Step>
