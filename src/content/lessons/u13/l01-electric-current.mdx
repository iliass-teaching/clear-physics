<Step kind="learn" title="Current is a flow of charge" minutes={14}>

- An **electric current** is a **flow of charge**. The bigger the current, the more charge passes a point each second.
- Current is measured in **amperes** (A), often called *amps*.
- In a **metal** wire, the charges that move are **free electrons** (Unit 12). When a cell is connected, all the free electrons in the circuit start to drift at the same time, so the lamp comes on at once.
- A current needs a **complete circuit**: a closed loop with no gaps.

<Figure svg={currentDirectionSvg()} caption="Conventional current and the flow of electrons." width="32rem" />

**Extended: which direction?** We say that **conventional current** flows from the **positive** terminal to the **negative** terminal, around the circuit. But electrons are negative: they flow the **other way**, from **negative** to **positive**. (The direction of current was chosen before electrons were discovered.)

</Step>

- **Direct current (d.c.)** flows in **one direction** only. Cells and batteries give d.c.
- **Alternating current (a.c.)** changes direction **again and again**. The mains supply is a.c. (in many countries, 50 times each second).

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Model a circuit" minutes={14}>

**The rope model:** stand in a circle and hold a long loop of rope loosely in your hands. One student (the cell) pulls the rope around.

1. When the "cell" pulls, does the rope move everywhere at the same time?
2. Hold the rope tightly in one place (a "lamp" with resistance). What do you feel? (Your hands get warm: energy is transferred.)
3. What in the model is like the electrons? What is like the current?

**Then:** build a circuit with a cell, a switch and a lamp. Open and close the switch. Draw the circuit, with an arrow for the conventional current.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** 4.2.2 C1, C3, C4 are Core. Conventional current versus electron flow (S6) is Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 24 min, model and circuit 14 min, check 12 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- "The current is used up in the lamp." The current is the same before and after the lamp; energy is transferred, not current.
- "Electrons travel very fast from the cell to the lamp." They drift slowly; all of them start moving at once.
- Mixing up the direction of conventional current and electron flow.

**Language support:**

- *current* = flow (like the current of a river).
- *alternating* = changing again and again from one to the other.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: a labelled circuit to copy, with arrows.
- Stretch: why does a lamp come on at once if the electrons move only a few millimetres each second?

**Safety:** use only cells or low-voltage supplies. Never experiment with the mains.

</Step>
