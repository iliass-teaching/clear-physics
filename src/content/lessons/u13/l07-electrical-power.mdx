- to the **components** (a lamp, a heater, a motor),
- and then to the **surroundings** (as light, sound, thermal energy, kinetic energy…).

<Step kind="learn" title="Power and energy" minutes={14}>

**Power** is the energy transferred each second (Unit 5). For an electrical component:

$$
P = IV
$$

- $P$: power (W); $I$: current (A); $V$: p.d. (V).

**Energy** transferred in a time $t$:

$$
E = IVt
$$

- $E$: energy (J); $t$: time (s). (This is $E = Pt$, with $P = IV$.)

<WorkedExample
  question="A kettle works at 230 V. The current is 10 A. It boils water in 3 minutes. Calculate (a) its power, (b) the energy transferred."
  given={['$V = 230\\,\\text{V}$', '$I = 10\\,\\text{A}$', '$t = 3\\,\\text{min} = 180\\,\\text{s}$']}
  find="the power $P$ and the energy $E$"
  answer="$P = 2300\,\text{W}$; $E = 414\,000\,\text{J}$"
>

(a) $P = IV = 10 \times 230 = 2300\,\text{W}$

(b) $E = IVt = 10 \times 230 \times 180 = 414\,000\,\text{J}$ (about $4.1 \times 10^5\,\text{J}$)

</WorkedExample>

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core:** 4.2.5 C1–C3.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, energy 8 min, equations 14 min, practice 25 min, exit ticket 8 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- Time in minutes in $E = IVt$.
- Confusing power (W) and energy (J).
- $P = \dfrac{V}{I}$ (this is resistance).

**Language support:**

- *rate* = how fast; power = the rate of energy transfer.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: level 1 only, with a table of $I$, $V$, $P$ to complete.
- Stretch: show that $P = I^2 R$ using $V = IR$. (Not required, but useful for Unit 15.)
