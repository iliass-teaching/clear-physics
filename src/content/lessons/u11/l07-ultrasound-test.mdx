<Step kind="learn" title="Ultrasound" minutes={12}>

**Ultrasound** is sound with a frequency **higher than 20 kHz** (20 000 Hz). Humans cannot hear it, but bats and dolphins use it.

**Extended: uses of ultrasound.** A short pulse of ultrasound is sent out. It reflects from a surface (an echo). We measure the time for the echo to come back.

<Figure svg={sonarSvg()} caption="Sonar: finding the depth of the sea." width="30rem" />

- **Sonar:** ships find the depth of the sea, or fish and submarines.
- **Medical scanning of soft tissue:** for example a baby inside its mother. Ultrasound is much safer than X-rays.
- **Non-destructive testing:** finding cracks inside metal (for example in railway tracks or aircraft parts) without breaking them.

**The calculation:** the pulse goes there **and back**:

$$
\text{depth} = \frac{\text{speed} \times \text{time}}{2}
$$

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** the definition of ultrasound (3.4 C9) is Core. Uses and calculations (S12) are Extended.

**Timing:** ultrasound 12 min, practice 15 min, unit test 33 min.

**Values:** ultrasound speeds used in the practice: about 1500 m/s in sea water, about 1540 m/s in soft tissue (the usual value for medical scanners), 5000 m/s in a steel (real values for steel are about 5000–6000 m/s).

**Using the test:** questions 1–8 test Core content; 9–10 are Extended.

**Common mistakes:**

- Forgetting to divide by 2.
- Times in µs or ms not changed to seconds.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: a worked example on the board before the practice.
- Stretch: why is ultrasound, not audible sound, used for medical scans? (Shorter wavelength: more detail.)

</Step>
