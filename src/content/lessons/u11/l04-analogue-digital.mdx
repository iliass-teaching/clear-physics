<Step kind="learn" title="Two kinds of signal" minutes={12}>

<Figure svg={analogueDigitalSvg()} caption="An analogue signal and a digital signal." width="32rem" />

- An **analogue** signal changes **smoothly** and can have **any value**. Example: the voltage from a microphone follows the sound wave.
- A **digital** signal has only **two values**: **1** (on, high) and **0** (off, low). Example: the signal in a computer.

**Sound** can be sent as an analogue signal (old telephones, AM and FM radio) or as a digital signal (mobile phones, digital radio, music streaming). To make a digital signal, the sound is measured many times each second and each measurement is sent as a number in 1s and 0s.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Why digital is better" minutes={14}>

Every signal picks up **noise** on the way (random changes). Over a long distance, it also becomes **weaker**, so it must be **amplified** (made bigger) again and again.

<Figure svg={regenerationSvg()} caption="Noise on a digital signal and on an analogue signal." width="34rem" />

- **Digital:** even with noise, it is easy to see what is a 1 and what is a 0. So the signal can be **regenerated**: made exactly like the start, with no noise. This can be repeated many times, so digital signals can travel **further** (increased **range**).
- **Analogue:** the amplifier cannot know which part is noise. The noise is amplified **with** the signal, and gets worse at each step.
- Digital signals can also carry **more data each second** (a higher **rate of transmission**).

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Pass the message" minutes={12}>

Work in a line of 6 students.

1. **Analogue:** the first student draws a wavy line on a card. Each student copies the line onto a new card **from memory** after looking for 3 seconds, and passes it on. Compare the last card with the first.
2. **Digital:** the first student writes a code of 12 digits (only 0 and 1). Each student copies it **from memory** after looking for 3 seconds, and passes it on. Compare.

Which message arrived with fewer errors? Why?

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Extended only** (3.3 S8–S10). Core students can do the "pass the message" activity and revise lessons 11.1–11.3.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 26 min, activity 12 min, check 12 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- "Digital signals do not get noise."
- "Analogue means old, digital means fast." Use the real definitions: any value / two values.

**Language support:**

- *analogue* (British spelling; *analog* in American English).
- *regenerate* = make again; *amplify* = make bigger.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: the two graphs to label "analogue" and "digital", with three sentence cards to place.
- Stretch: a 3-minute song is sent as 44 100 numbers per second, each of 16 bits, in two channels. Estimate the number of bits.

</Step>
