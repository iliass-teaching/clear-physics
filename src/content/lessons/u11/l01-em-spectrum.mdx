<Step kind="learn" title="A family of waves" minutes={14}>

Visible light is one member of a big family: the **electromagnetic spectrum**.

<Figure svg={emSpectrumSvg()} caption="The electromagnetic spectrum." width="38rem" />

In order of **increasing frequency** (and **decreasing wavelength**):

**radio waves → microwaves → infrared → visible light → ultraviolet → X-rays → gamma rays**

A memory sentence: **R**abbits **M**ate **I**n **V**ery **U**nusual e**X**pensive **G**ardens.

What all electromagnetic waves have in common:

- They are **transverse** waves.
- They can travel through a **vacuum** (empty space).
- They all travel at the **same speed** in a vacuum: $3.0 \times 10^8$ m/s. In air, the speed is almost the same.
- They obey $v = f\lambda$. So a **higher frequency** means a **shorter wavelength**.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Calculations" minutes={8}>

<WorkedExample
  question="A radio station broadcasts at 100 MHz. Calculate the wavelength of its radio waves."
  given={['$f = 100\\,\\text{MHz} = 1.00 \\times 10^8\\,\\text{Hz}$', '$v = 3.0 \\times 10^8\\,\\text{m/s}$']}
  find="the wavelength $\lambda$"
  answer="$\lambda = 3.0\,\text{m}$"
>

$v = f\lambda$, so $\lambda = \dfrac{v}{f}$

$\lambda = \dfrac{3.0 \times 10^8}{1.00 \times 10^8}$

$\lambda = 3.0\,\text{m}$

</WorkedExample>

**Units:** 1 MHz = $10^6$ Hz; 1 GHz = $10^9$ Hz; 1 nm = $10^{-9}$ m.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** the order of the regions (3.3 C1) and the same speed in a vacuum (C2) are Core. The value $3.0 \times 10^8$ m/s and "approximately the same in air" (S6) are Extended; Core questions give the value.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 22 min, explore 10 min, practice 18 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**About the wavelengths:** the boundaries between regions are not sharp and books give different values. The values in the interactive spectrum are typical. The exam asks for the **order**, not exact boundaries.

**Common mistakes:**

- Putting microwaves after infrared, or X-rays before ultraviolet.
- Thinking that higher frequency waves travel faster.
- Forgetting MHz/GHz or nm conversions.

**Language support:**

- *spectrum* = the full range, in order.
- Sentence frame: *"… has a higher frequency than …, so it has a shorter wavelength."*

**Differentiation:**

- Support: a strip of seven cards to put in order.
- Stretch: which region has wavelengths about the size of an atom? (X-rays: about $10^{-10}$ m.)

</Step>
