<Step kind="learn" title="Satellites" minutes={14}>

Communication with **artificial satellites** is mainly by **microwaves**: they pass easily through the atmosphere.

<Figure svg={satellitesSvg()} caption="A low-orbit satellite and a geostationary satellite (not to scale)." width="32rem" />

**Geostationary satellites:**

- about 36 000 km above the equator;
- one orbit takes **24 hours**, the same time as the Earth's spin, so the satellite stays **above the same place**;
- dishes on the ground can point at it and stay still;
- used for **direct broadcast satellite television** and some **satellite phones**.

**Low-orbit satellites:**

- a few hundred to about 2000 km high; they go around the Earth in about 1.5 to 2 hours;
- the signal has a shorter distance to travel, so there is less delay;
- many satellites are needed to cover the Earth; used by some **satellite phones**.

</Step>

| System | Waves | Why? |
|---|---|---|
| mobile phones, wireless internet (Wi-Fi) | **microwaves** | they can pass through some walls; they need only a **short aerial** |
| Bluetooth | **radio waves** | they pass through walls, but the signal becomes weaker: good for short distances |
| cable TV, high-speed broadband | **visible light** or **infrared** in **optical fibres** | glass is transparent to them; they can carry **a lot of data** each second |

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** satellites (3.3 C5) are Core. Mobile phones, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and optical fibres (S7) are Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, satellites 14 min, Extended table 12 min, practice 18 min, exit ticket 11 min.

**Numbers:** geostationary orbit: about 36 000 km above the equator, period 24 hours. Low orbits: about 160 km to 2000 km, periods of about 90–130 minutes. These are typical values, not syllabus requirements.

**Bluetooth:** it works at about 2.4 GHz (wavelength about 12.5 cm). With the usual boundary (microwaves from about 1 mm to 1 m), this is in the microwave region. The syllabus lists Bluetooth under radio waves: the boundary between radio waves and microwaves is not sharp. Follow the syllabus in exam answers.

**Common mistakes:**

- "A geostationary satellite does not move."
- "Satellites use sound waves."

**Language support:**

- *geo* = Earth; *stationary* = not moving (it does not move compared to the ground).
- *aerial* = antenna.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: a diagram of the two orbits to label.
- Stretch: why do phone calls through a geostationary satellite have a small delay?

</Step>
