<Step kind="learn" title="What is momentum?" minutes={12}>

**Momentum** is mass × velocity:

$$
p = m \times v
$$

<div class="table-wrap">

| Quantity | Symbol | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| momentum | $p$ | kg m/s |
| mass | $m$ | kg |
| velocity | $v$ | m/s |

</div>

An object has a big momentum if it has a big mass, or a big velocity, or both. An object at rest has **zero** momentum.

<WorkedExample
  question="A truck of mass 12 000 kg moves at 5 m/s. Calculate its momentum."
  given={['$m = 12\\,000\\,\\text{kg}$', '$v = 5\\,\\text{m/s}$']}
  find="the momentum $p$"
  answer="$p = 60\,000\,\text{kg m/s}$"
>

$p = m \times v = 12\,000 \times 5$

$p = 60\,000\,\text{kg m/s}$

</WorkedExample>

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Momentum has a direction" minutes={10}>

Velocity is a vector, so **momentum is a vector** too. In a straight line, we choose one direction as **positive**, for example to the right. Then:

- an object moving to the right has a **positive** momentum;
- an object moving to the left has a **negative** momentum.

<Figure svg={collisionSvg([{ m: 2, v: 3, label: 'A' }, { m: 1, v: -4, label: 'B' }])} caption="A moves right, B moves left." width="28rem" />

- A: $p = 2 \times (+3) = +6\,\text{kg m/s}$
- B: $p = 1 \times (-4) = -4\,\text{kg m/s}$
- total momentum: $+6 + (-4) = +2\,\text{kg m/s}$, so $2\,\text{kg m/s}$ to the right.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**This unit is Extended content** (1.6 S1–S4). Core students do not need it for the exam.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 22 min, table 10 min, practice 18 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- Forgetting the minus sign for objects moving the other way.
- Using grams instead of kilograms.
- Writing the unit as N or kg/m/s.

**Table answers:** tennis ball 2.85; football 10.75; sprinter 700; car 15 600; ship 1.0 × 10⁹ kg m/s.

**Language support:**

- In everyday English, *momentum* also means "the energy to keep going" (a team "has momentum"). In physics it is $mv$.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: give the masses in kg and avoid negative velocities at first.
- Stretch: which has more momentum: a 60 kg runner at 8 m/s or a 0.01 kg bullet at 400 m/s? (Runner 480, bullet 4 kg m/s.)

**Starter answer:** parked truck (zero), gentle tennis ball, fast tennis ball, bicycle, truck at 5 m/s.

</Step>
