<Step kind="learn" title="The principle" minutes={10}>

When objects collide, or push each other apart, and **no outside force** acts on them:

$$
\text{total momentum before} = \text{total momentum after}
$$

This is the **principle of conservation of momentum**. Momentum is not lost: it is passed from one object to another.

<Figure svg={collisionSvg([{ m: 2, v: 6, label: 'A' }, { m: 1, v: 0, label: 'B' }])} caption="Before: A moves at 6 m/s, B is at rest." width="28rem" />

<WorkedExample
  question="Trolley A (2 kg) moves at 6 m/s. It hits trolley B (1 kg), which is at rest. They stick together. Calculate their velocity after the collision."
  given={['A: $2\\,\\text{kg}$ at $6\\,\\text{m/s}$', 'B: $1\\,\\text{kg}$ at rest', 'they stick together']}
  find="the velocity $v$ after the collision"
  answer="$v = 4\,\text{m/s}$"
>

momentum before $= 2 \times 6 + 1 \times 0 = 12\,\text{kg m/s}$

momentum after $= (2 + 1) \times v = 3v$

$3v = 12$, so $v = 4\,\text{m/s}$

</WorkedExample>

**Check:** after the collision there is more mass moving, so the velocity is smaller.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Recoil" minutes={8}>

When two objects at rest **push apart** (an explosion, a gun firing, a skater pushing a ball), the total momentum before is **zero**. So the total momentum after is also zero:

$$
m_A v_A + m_B v_B = 0
$$

The two objects move in **opposite directions**. The lighter object moves faster.

Example: a cannon (1000 kg) fires a cannonball (10 kg) forwards at 200 m/s.
$10 \times 200 + 1000 \times v = 0$, so $v = -2\,\text{m/s}$: the cannon recoils (moves backwards) at 2 m/s.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Extended content** (1.6 S3): simple problems in **one dimension** only.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 18 min, simulation 15 min, practice 17 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- Dividing by one mass instead of the total mass after a sticking collision.
- Forgetting the minus sign when an object moves the other way.
- Thinking momentum is "used up" in a collision.

**About the simulation:** the trolleys always stick together. Energy is not conserved in this kind of collision (some kinetic energy becomes heating and sound), but momentum is. Extended students can check this with $E_k = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$ after Unit 5.

**Language support:**

- *Conserved* = the total stays the same. Show with coins: pass them between students, the total does not change.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: Level 1 (one trolley at rest).
- Stretch: two trolleys of the same mass and the same speed hit head-on and stick. What is their velocity after? (Zero.)

</Step>
