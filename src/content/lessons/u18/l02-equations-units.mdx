<Step kind="learn" title="Prefixes and conversions" minutes={10}>

<div class="table-wrap">

| Prefix | Symbol | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| nano | n | $10^{-9}$ | 500 nm $= 5 \times 10^{-7}$ m |
| micro | µ | $10^{-6}$ | 20 µs $= 2 \times 10^{-5}$ s |
| milli | m | $10^{-3}$ | 250 mA $= 0.25$ A |
| kilo | k | $10^{3}$ | 2.3 kW $= 2300$ W |
| mega | M | $10^{6}$ | 100 MHz $= 1.0 \times 10^8$ Hz |
| giga | G | $10^{9}$ | 2.4 GHz $= 2.4 \times 10^9$ Hz |

</div>

**Areas and volumes:** $1\,\text{m}^2 = 10\,000\,\text{cm}^2$; $1\,\text{m}^3 = 1\,000\,000\,\text{cm}^3$. (1 m = 100 cm, so square it or cube it.)

**Time:** 1 h = 60 min = 3600 s.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Source:** the equation list was made from every "recall and use" learning point of the syllabus. The "Values to know" come from the syllabus too (for example $g \approx 9.8$ m/s², 330–350 m/s for sound). The mathematical requirements (section 4 of the syllabus) include unit conversions such as cm³ to m³.

**Timing:** starter 7 min, equations 15 min, prefixes 10 min, practice 20 min, exit ticket 8 min.

**Tip:** give each student a printed equation sheet without the equations (only the words) to complete from memory, once a week until the exam.

**Common mistakes:**

- Using the same letter for two quantities: $p$ (pressure, momentum), $P$ (power), $E$ (energy, e.m.f.). Look at the context.
- Converting cm² or cm³ with 100 instead of 10 000 or 1 000 000.

</Step>
