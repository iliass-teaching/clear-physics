Two students test which ball bounces higher. One drops a tennis ball from 1 m onto a carpet; the other drops a rubber ball from 2 m onto a wooden floor. Is it a fair test? What should they change?

<Step kind="learn" title="Planning an experiment" minutes={14}>

- **Independent variable:** the one **you change** (for example the length of a wire).
- **Dependent variable:** the one **you measure** (for example its resistance).
- **Control variables:** the ones you **keep the same**, so that the test is fair (the same metal, thickness, temperature…). Say **how** you keep each one constant.
- **Number and range:** use at least **5** different values, spread over a wide range (for example 20 cm, 40 cm, 60 cm, 80 cm, 100 cm). **Repeat** each reading and take the mean.
- **Method:** numbered steps, with the apparatus, what you measure and how.
- **Safety:** name a hazard and a precaution (hot wire: switch off between readings; heavy masses: keep feet away).
- **Results and conclusion:** say how you will record (table) and process them (graph, gradient), and what pattern would support your prediction.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Evaluating results" minutes={12}>

<div class="table-wrap">

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **accurate** | close to the true value |
| **precise** | the repeated values are close to each other |
| **anomaly** | a result outside the pattern of the others |
| **random error** | unpredictable scatter (for example reaction time); reduce it by repeating and taking a mean |
| **systematic error** | the same error each time (for example a zero error); repeating does not help — correct it |
| **range** | from the smallest to the biggest value of a variable |

</div>

**Do two values agree?** At IGCSE, two results agree "within the limits of experimental accuracy" if they differ by **10% or less**.

**Improvements:** suggest a **specific** change and say **why** it helps: "use a longer length of wire, so the p.d. readings are bigger and the percentage error is smaller", not just "be more careful".

</Step>

<ExamQuestion number="1" marks={6}>

Plan an experiment to find how the resistance of a wire depends on its length. You have: a long constantan wire on a metre rule, a power supply, an ammeter, a voltmeter, crocodile clips and leads.

In your plan, include: the variables, the method, a table for the results, how you will use the results, and one safety precaution.

<div slot="scheme">

Any six of:

- independent variable: length; dependent variable: resistance (from V and I) **[1]**
- control: the same wire (material, thickness), temperature: small current / switch off between readings **[1]**
- circuit: ammeter in series, voltmeter across the wire between the clips **[1]**
- at least 5 lengths over a wide range (for example 20 cm to 100 cm) **[1]**
- repeat and take the mean **[1]**
- table with headings length / cm, V / V, I / A, R / Ω **[1]**
- calculate $R = \dfrac{V}{I}$ and plot R against length; a straight line through the origin shows R ∝ length **[1]**
- safety: the wire can get hot — keep the current small, switch off between readings **[1]**
