<Step kind="learn" title="Readings and tables" minutes={14}>

**Taking readings:**

- Read an analogue scale to the nearest **half** of the smallest division.
- Look straight at the scale (eye level), to avoid a **parallax** error.
- Check for a **zero error**: what does the instrument read when nothing is measured? Correct value = reading − zero error.
- Time many oscillations (for example 10), then divide: the reaction time error becomes smaller.

**Recording readings in a table:**

- Each column heading has the **quantity** and the **unit**, separated by a solidus: **time / s**.
- **No units** in the body of the table.
- Give each reading to the **precision of the instrument** (for example a ruler with mm divisions: 23.5 cm, not 23.48 cm, not 24 cm). Keep the same number of decimal places in a column.
- **Repeat** readings where you can, and calculate the **mean**. Leave out **anomalous** results.
- A calculated value has the same number of significant figures as the **least** precise data used (2 or 3).

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Graphs" minutes={12}>

- **Axes:** label each axis with the quantity and unit (as in the table: **time / s**). The independent variable is usually on the horizontal axis.
- **Scales:** use more than **half** of the grid in both directions, with easy steps (1, 2 or 5 units per 2 cm, or 10, 20, 50…). Do not use steps of 3.
- **Points:** plot each point accurately (to half a small square), as a small cross (×), a plus (+) or a circled dot (⊙). Use a sharp pencil.
- **Best-fit line:** one **thin**, **smooth** line (straight or curved) with the points spread evenly on both sides. It does not have to go through every point. Ignore clear anomalies.
- **Gradient:** draw a big triangle on the line, at least **half the length** of the line; gradient = $\dfrac{\text{change in } y}{\text{change in } x}$, to 2 or 3 significant figures, with units.
- **Intercept:** where the line crosses an axis; extend the line (extrapolate) if needed.

</Step>
