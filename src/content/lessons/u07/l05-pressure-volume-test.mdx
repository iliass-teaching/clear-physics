<Step kind="learn" title="Pressure × volume = constant (Extended)" minutes={10}>

A syringe holds a fixed mass of air. We push the plunger slowly, so the temperature does not change. We measure the pressure:

<div class="table-wrap">

| volume $V$ / cm³ | 50 | 40 | 25 | 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pressure $p$ / kPa | 100 | 125 | 200 | 250 |
| $p \times V$ | 5000 | 5000 | 5000 | 5000 |

</div>

$p \times V$ is the **same** every time. For a **fixed mass of gas** at **constant temperature**:

$$
pV = \text{constant} \qquad \text{so} \qquad p_1 V_1 = p_2 V_2
$$

- Half the volume → double the pressure.
- Double the volume → half the pressure.

<WorkedExample
  question="A gas has a volume of 30 cm³ at a pressure of 200 kPa. It is compressed to 12 cm³. The temperature does not change. Calculate the new pressure."
  given={['$p_1 = 200\\,\\text{kPa}$', '$V_1 = 30\\,\\text{cm}^3$', '$V_2 = 12\\,\\text{cm}^3$']}
  find="the new pressure $p_2$"
  answer="$p_2 = 500\,\text{kPa}$"
>

$p_1 V_1 = p_2 V_2$, so $p_2 = \dfrac{p_1 V_1}{V_2}$

$p_2 = \dfrac{200 \times 30}{12}$

$p_2 = 500\,\text{kPa}$

**Check:** the volume is smaller, so the pressure must be bigger. ✓

</WorkedExample>

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** $pV = \text{constant}$ (2.1.3 S3) is Extended. Core students can spend the first 25 minutes on the kelvin practice and on revising lessons 1–4 (for example with the simulations).

**Timing:** starter 3 min, learn 10 min, practice 15 min, unit test 30 min, plus 2 min to start.

**Using the test:** questions 1–8 test Core content; 9–10 are Extended.

**Common mistakes:**

- Using $p_1 V_2 = p_2 V_1$ (the pressure changes the wrong way). Teach students to check: smaller volume → bigger pressure.
- Forgetting the conditions: *fixed mass of gas*, *constant temperature*.
- Mixing units: $p_1$ and $p_2$ must be in the same unit, and $V_1$ and $V_2$ too.

**Possible practical (demonstration):** a Boyle's law apparatus (oil column with a pressure gauge and a bicycle pump), or a sealed syringe on a pressure sensor. Change the volume slowly, so the temperature stays the same.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: give the first line of each calculation: $p_1 V_1 = p_2 V_2$.
- Stretch: plot $p$ against $\dfrac{1}{V}$ for the table above. What shape is the graph? Why?

</Step>
