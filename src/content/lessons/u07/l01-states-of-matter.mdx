Put these in three groups: **solid**, **liquid** or **gas**.

> ice · steam · milk · air · sand · oil · a coin · the helium in a balloon

<Step kind="learn" title="Three states of matter" minutes={12}>

Matter can be in three **states**: solid, liquid or gas.

<div class="table-wrap">

| | Shape | Volume | Can you compress it? | Does it flow? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **solid** | fixed | fixed | almost no | no |
| **liquid** | takes the shape of the container | fixed | almost no | yes |
| **gas** | fills all of the container | not fixed: fills the container | yes, easily | yes |

</div>

- **Compress** means *push into a smaller volume*.
- Sand is a solid: each grain has a fixed shape and a fixed volume. Many small grains can flow together.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Changes of state" minutes={10}>

<Figure svg={changesOfStateSvg()} caption="The changes of state. Heating: blue arrows. Cooling: red arrows." width="34rem" />

<div class="table-wrap">

| Change | From → to | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **melting** | solid → liquid | ice melts in a drink |
| **freezing** (solidification) | liquid → solid | water freezes in a freezer |
| **boiling** | liquid → gas, everywhere in the liquid | water boils in a kettle |
| **evaporation** | liquid → gas, at the surface only | wet clothes dry in the sun |
| **condensation** | gas → liquid | drops of water on a cold bottle |
| **sublimation** | solid → gas, without melting | dry ice (solid carbon dioxide) becomes gas |
| **deposition** | gas → solid, without condensing first | frost forms on a cold window in winter |

</div>

For water: ice melts at **0 °C** and water boils at **100 °C** (at normal air pressure).

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Test the three states" minutes={15}>

**Equipment:** two plastic syringes (no needle), water, a small piece of modelling clay, a beaker.

1. **Air:** pull the plunger out to 20 cm³. Put your finger on the end. Push the plunger. What happens to the volume?
2. **Water:** fill a syringe with 20 cm³ of water. Put your finger on the end. Push. What happens?
3. **Solid:** try to squeeze the modelling clay into a smaller volume. Does its shape change? Does its volume change?
4. Pour the water from the syringe into the beaker. Does it keep its shape? Its volume?

**Write one sentence for each state.** Start with: *"A gas can be compressed because …"* (you will see why in the next lesson).

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 22 min, activity 15 min, check 13 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Syllabus note:** the changes gas → solid (deposition) and solid → gas (sublimation) are not required (2.1.1 C2). They are shown so that the picture is complete, but they are not tested here.

**Common mistakes:**

- "A gas has no volume." It has a volume: the volume of its container.
- Mixing up *evaporation* and *condensation*. Link them to what students see: evaporation = puddles drying; condensation = water drops on a cold can.
- Saying that steam is the white cloud above a kettle. The cloud is small drops of liquid water (the gas has condensed). Steam itself is invisible.

**Language support:**

- Word pairs: *melt / freeze*, *evaporate / condense*. The noun ends in *-ing* or *-tion*: *melting*, *condensation*.
- *State* has other meanings in English (a part of a country; to say). In physics, *state of matter* means solid, liquid or gas.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: give the table with some empty cells to fill in.
- Stretch: why do wet clothes dry faster on a windy day? (Taught in Unit 8: evaporation.)

**Safety:** syringes without needles only. Wipe up spilled water.

</Step>
