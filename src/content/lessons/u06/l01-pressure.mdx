<Step kind="learn" title="What is pressure?" minutes={12}>

**Pressure** is the force on each unit of area:

$$
p = \frac{F}{A}
$$

<div class="table-wrap">

| Quantity | Symbol | Units |
|---|---|---|
| pressure | $p$ | N/m² (= pascal, Pa) or N/cm² |
| force | $F$ | N |
| area | $A$ | m² or cm² |

</div>

- The **same force** on a **smaller area** gives a **bigger pressure**.
- A **bigger force** on the same area gives a bigger pressure.

<Figure svg={brickSvg()} caption="The same brick: standing up, it presses harder on the ground." width="32rem" />

<WorkedExample
  question="A box has a weight of 600 N. It stands on an area of 0.30 m². Calculate the pressure on the floor."
  given={['$F = 600\\,\\text{N}$', '$A = 0.30\\,\\text{m}^2$']}
  find="the pressure $p$"
  answer="$p = 2000\,\text{N/m}^2 = 2000\,\text{Pa}$"
>

$p = \dfrac{F}{A} = \dfrac{600}{0.30}$

$p = 2000\,\text{N/m}^2$

</WorkedExample>

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Pressure in everyday life" minutes={8}>

<div class="table-wrap">

| We want a **big** pressure: small area | We want a **small** pressure: big area |
|---|---|
| a sharp knife cuts easily | snowshoes stop you sinking into snow |
| a drawing pin goes into a board | wide tyres on a tractor do not sink into mud |
| the studs on football boots grip the grass | a camel’s wide feet do not sink into sand |
| a nail has a sharp point | the wide straps of a heavy bag do not hurt your shoulders |

</div>

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Your pressure on the floor" minutes={12}>

1. Find your mass $m$ (in kg). Calculate your weight: $F = mg$ (use $g = 9.8\,\text{N/kg}$).
2. Stand on squared paper (1 cm squares). Draw around your shoe.
3. Count the squares inside the outline (count a square if more than half of it is inside). This is the area of one shoe in cm².
4. Calculate the pressure when you stand on **two feet**, then on **one foot**, in N/cm².

**Question:** why does the pressure double when you stand on one foot?

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 20 min, activity 12 min, practice 18 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Units:** the syllabus uses N/m² and N/cm² at Core, and the pascal (Pa) at Extended. $1\,\text{Pa} = 1\,\text{N/m}^2$.

**Common mistakes:**

- Using the mass (kg) as the force: the force is the weight, $mg$.
- Changing cm² to m² by dividing by 100 (it is 10 000).
- Multiplying force and area.

**Language support:**

- In everyday English, *pressure* can mean stress ("I am under pressure"). In physics it is force per unit area.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: use areas in m² only at first.
- Stretch: an elephant (40 000 N on 4 feet of 0.12 m² each) or a person in high heels (600 N on 1 cm²): who puts more pressure on the floor? (Elephant: about 83 000 Pa; high heel: 6 000 000 Pa.)

**Safety:** use a pencil gently in the starter, without a very sharp point.

</Step>
