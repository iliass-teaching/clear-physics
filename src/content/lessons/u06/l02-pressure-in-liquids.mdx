<Step kind="learn" title="Depth and density" minutes={12}>

The pressure in a liquid is caused by the **weight of the liquid above**.

- The pressure **increases with depth**: deeper down, there is more liquid above.
- The pressure is bigger in a **denser** liquid: at the same depth, the liquid above is heavier.
- At one depth, the pressure acts **in all directions**.

<Step kind="learn" title="Calculating the pressure (Extended)" minutes={10}>

The change in pressure beneath the surface of a liquid:

$$
\Delta p = \rho g \Delta h
$$

- $\Delta p$: change in pressure (Pa)
- $\rho$: density of the liquid (kg/m³) — water: $1000\,\text{kg/m}^3$
- $g$: gravitational field strength ($9.8\,\text{N/kg}$)
- $\Delta h$: change in depth (m)

<WorkedExample
  question="Calculate the extra pressure due to the water at the bottom of a swimming pool 2.5 m deep."
  given={['$\\rho = 1000\\,\\text{kg/m}^3$', '$g = 9.8\\,\\text{N/kg}$', '$\\Delta h = 2.5\\,\\text{m}$']}
  find="the change in pressure $\Delta p$"
  answer="$\Delta p = 24\,500\,\text{Pa}$"
>

$\Delta p = \rho g \Delta h = 1000 \times 9.8 \times 2.5$

$\Delta p = 24\,500\,\text{Pa}$

</WorkedExample>

This is the pressure **due to the water only**. The air above also pushes on the surface (atmospheric pressure, about 100 000 Pa).

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Holes in a bottle" minutes={13}>

**Equipment:** a tall plastic bottle with 3 holes at different heights (covered with tape), a tray, water, a ruler.

1. Fill the bottle with water. Put it on the edge of the tray.
2. Remove the tapes at the same time.
3. Watch the jets of water. Measure how far each jet reaches on the tray.

**Questions:**

1. Which jet comes out fastest? Explain using *depth* and *pressure*.
2. Predict what happens if you use salty water (a denser liquid) at the same depths.
3. As the bottle empties, what happens to the jets? Why?

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** the qualitative ideas (1.8 C3) are Core. $\Delta p = \rho g \Delta h$ (1.8 S4) is Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 22 min, bottle 13 min, practice 15 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- Using the density in g/cm³ (use kg/m³).
- Thinking the pressure depends on the shape or the width of the container (it depends on depth and density only).
- Adding the atmospheric pressure when the question asks for the change in pressure.

**About the bottle:** the lowest jet comes out fastest, because the pressure is biggest there. How far each jet lands also depends on the height of the hole above the tray, so ask students to compare how fast the water comes out.

**Language support:**

- Sentence frame: *"The deeper the water, the … the pressure, because …"*

**Differentiation:**

- Support: focus on the bottle and the dam.
- Stretch: the deepest point of the ocean is about 11 000 m. Estimate the pressure due to the sea water there ($\rho = 1030\,\text{kg/m}^3$). (About $1.1 \times 10^8\,\text{Pa}$.)

**Safety:** do the bottle experiment over a tray or a sink. Wipe up spills.

</Step>
