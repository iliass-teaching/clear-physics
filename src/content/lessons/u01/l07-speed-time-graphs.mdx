<Step kind="learn" title="Reading a speed–time graph" minutes={10}>

On a speed–time graph, **speed** is on the vertical axis.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Shape of the line | What the object does |
|---|---|
| flat, on the time axis (speed = 0) | at rest |
| flat, above the time axis | constant speed |
| straight, going up | speeds up at a constant rate (constant acceleration) |
| straight, going down | slows down at a constant rate (constant deceleration) |
| curve | the acceleration changes (Extended) |

</div>

<Step kind="learn" title="Acceleration (Extended)" minutes={10}>

**Acceleration** is the change in velocity per unit time.

$$
a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}
$$

- $\Delta v$ (say "delta v") means **change in velocity**: $\Delta v = \text{final velocity} - \text{start velocity}$.
- $\Delta t$ means **change in time** (the time taken).
- The unit of acceleration is **m/s²** ("metres per second squared").

An acceleration of $2\,\text{m/s}^2$ means: the speed increases by 2 m/s **every second**.

<WorkedExample
  question="A car starts from rest. It reaches 20 m/s in 8 s. Calculate its acceleration."
  given={['start speed $= 0\\,\\text{m/s}$', 'final speed $= 20\\,\\text{m/s}$', 'time $\\Delta t = 8\\,\\text{s}$']}
  find="the acceleration $a$"
  answer="$a = 2.5\,\text{m/s}^2$"
>

$\Delta v = 20 - 0 = 20\,\text{m/s}$

$a = \dfrac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \dfrac{20}{8} = 2.5\,\text{m/s}^2$

</WorkedExample>

When an object slows down, $\Delta v$ is negative, so the acceleration is negative.
A negative acceleration is a **deceleration**.

<WorkedExample
  question="The car brakes from 20 m/s to rest in 4 s. Calculate its acceleration."
  given={['start speed $= 20\\,\\text{m/s}$', 'final speed $= 0\\,\\text{m/s}$', 'time $\\Delta t = 4\\,\\text{s}$']}
  find="the acceleration $a$"
  answer="$a = -5\,\text{m/s}^2$ (a deceleration of $5\,\text{m/s}^2$)"
>

$\Delta v = 0 - 20 = -20\,\text{m/s}$

$a = \dfrac{-20}{4} = -5\,\text{m/s}^2$

</WorkedExample>

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** shapes of speed–time graphs (1.2 C4, C5) are Core. Acceleration calculations and changing acceleration (S9, S10, S12) are Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 20 min, plotting 15 min, practice 15 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- Confusing distance–time and speed–time graphs (always read the axis labels first).
- Using the final speed instead of the change in speed.
- Writing a deceleration as a positive acceleration.
- Writing the unit as m/s instead of m/s².

**Answers for the plotting task:** 0–6 s: speeds up, $a = 2.5\,\text{m/s}^2$; 6–10 s: constant speed, $a = 0$; 10–14 s: slows down, $a = -3.75\,\text{m/s}^2$.

**Language support:**

- Read $\Delta$ as "change in". Write $\Delta v = \text{final} - \text{start}$ on the board all lesson.
- Sentence frame: *"The speed increases by … m/s every second, so the acceleration is … m/s²."*

**Differentiation:**

- Support: start with objects that start from rest, so $\Delta v$ = final speed.
- Stretch: sketch the speed–time graph of a ball thrown straight up, until it comes back to your hand. (Speed decreases to zero at the top, then increases.)

</Step>
