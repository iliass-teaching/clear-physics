<Step kind="learn" title="Distance = area under the graph" minutes={12}>

On a speed–time graph:

$$
\text{distance travelled} = \text{area under the graph}
$$

The area is between the line and the time axis. Check the units: $\text{m/s} \times \text{s} = \text{m}$.

- **Rectangle** (constant speed): $\text{area} = \text{base} \times \text{height}$.
- **Triangle** (constant acceleration from rest, or deceleration to rest): $\text{area} = \tfrac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$.
- **Other shapes:** split them into rectangles and triangles. Add the areas.

<WorkedExample
  question="Use the graph of the bus. Calculate the total distance."
  given={['A: triangle, base $4\\,\\text{s}$, height $8\\,\\text{m/s}$', 'B: rectangle, base $10\\,\\text{s}$, height $8\\,\\text{m/s}$', 'C: triangle, base $4\\,\\text{s}$, height $8\\,\\text{m/s}$']}
  find="the total distance between the two stops"
  answer="$112\,\text{m}$"
>

$A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 8 = 16\,\text{m}$

$B = 10 \times 8 = 80\,\text{m}$

$C = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 8 = 16\,\text{m}$

$\text{total distance} = 16 + 80 + 16 = 112\,\text{m}$

</WorkedExample>

<Step kind="learn" title="Acceleration = gradient (Extended)" minutes={8}>

On a speed–time graph, the gradient of the line **is the acceleration**:

$$
a = \text{gradient} = \frac{\text{change in speed}}{\text{change in time}} = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}
$$

For the bus:

- part A: $a = \dfrac{8 - 0}{4 - 0} = 2\,\text{m/s}^2$
- part B: the line is flat, so $a = 0$
- part C: $a = \dfrac{0 - 8}{18 - 14} = -2\,\text{m/s}^2$ (a deceleration)

**Remember:** area → distance. Gradient → acceleration.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** distance from the area (1.2 C7) is Core, for constant speed or constant acceleration. Acceleration from the gradient (1.2 S11) is Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 20 min, activity 15 min, practice 15 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- Using $\text{base} \times \text{height}$ for a triangle (forgetting the $\tfrac{1}{2}$).
- Calculating the gradient when the question asks for the distance, or the opposite.
- Using the whole rectangle under the graph instead of splitting the shape.

**Language support:**

- Keep a poster on the wall: "area → distance; gradient → acceleration".
- Shade the area with a highlighter on printed graphs.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: rectangles only, then one triangle.
- Stretch: two cars start together. Car A moves at a constant 10 m/s. Car B starts from rest with an acceleration of 2 m/s². When do they have the same speed? Who is in front at that moment? (5 s; car A: 50 m, car B: 25 m.)

</Step>
