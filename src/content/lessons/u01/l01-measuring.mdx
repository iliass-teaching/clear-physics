<Step kind="learn" title="Measuring length" minutes={6}>

We measure **length** with a ruler or a tape measure.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Unit | Symbol | |
|---|---|---|
| millimetre | mm | 10 mm = 1 cm |
| centimetre | cm | 100 cm = 1 m |
| metre | m | 1000 m = 1 km |

</div>

How to use a ruler:

1. Put the object next to the **scale**.
2. Look straight down at the scale, not from the side.
3. Read the start and the end of the object.
4. $\text{length} = \text{end} - \text{start}$

The end of a ruler is often damaged. So you can start at 1 cm, not at 0. Then do not forget to subtract.

<WorkedExample
  question="An object starts at the 2.0 cm line and ends at the 9.4 cm line. Calculate its length."
  given={['start $= 2.0\\,\\text{cm}$', 'end $= 9.4\\,\\text{cm}$']}
  find="the length"
  answer="$7.4\,\text{cm}$"
>

$\text{length} = \text{end} - \text{start}$

$\text{length} = 9.4 - 2.0 = 7.4\,\text{cm}$

</WorkedExample>

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Measuring the volume of a liquid" minutes={6}>

We measure the **volume** of a liquid with a **measuring cylinder**.
The unit is cm³ (cubic centimetre): $1000\,\text{cm}^3 = 1\,\text{dm}^3$.

The top of the liquid is curved. How to read it:

1. Put the cylinder on a flat table.
2. Put your eyes at the same height as the liquid.
3. Read the **bottom** of the curved surface.

**What is one small line worth?** Look at two numbers on the scale.
Divide the difference by the number of spaces between them.

Example: from 10 cm³ to 20 cm³ there are 5 spaces. So one small line $= \dfrac{20 - 10}{5} = 2\,\text{cm}^3$.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Measuring time" minutes={3}>

We measure **time** with a stopwatch, a clock or a digital timer.
The unit is the second (s): $1\,\text{min} = 60\,\text{s}$ and $1\,\text{h} = 60\,\text{min} = 3600\,\text{s}$.

When you use a stopwatch, you need some time to react. This **reaction time** is about 0.2 s.
For a short time, this is a big problem. The next lesson shows how to make it smaller.

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Measurement stations" minutes={20}>

Work in groups of 3. Spend about 6 minutes at each station.

**Station 1 — Length.** Measure the length of 3 objects (a pencil, a book, a leaf). Use the start-and-end method.

**Station 2 — Volume.** Your teacher gives you 3 containers of water. Pour each one into a measuring cylinder and read the volume.

**Station 3 — Time.** Let a ball roll down a ramp. Time it from the top to the bottom. Do it 3 times.

Write your results in tables. Put the unit in the column heading, like this:

<div class="table-wrap">

| object | length / cm |
|---|---|
| pencil | |
| book | |
| leaf | |

</div>

**Question:** at station 3, are your 3 times the same? Why not?
*Start your answer like this: "The times are different because …"*

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 15 min, stations 20 min, practice 15 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- Reading only the end of the object when it does not start at 0.
- Looking at the scale from the side (parallax error).
- Reading the top of the curved surface in a measuring cylinder.
- Thinking each small line is worth 1, without checking the scale.
- Treating 1 minute as 100 seconds.

**Language support:**

- Teach *scale* and *reading* with a real ruler in your hand: "This is the scale. 7.4 cm is the reading."
- Sentence frame for results: *"The length of the … is … cm."*

**Differentiation:**

- Support: give rulers that start at 0 first, then move to the start-and-end method.
- Stretch: compare the precision of a ruler (1 mm) and a measuring cylinder (2 cm³). Which reading is more precise?

**Safety:** glass measuring cylinders can break. Keep them on the table, away from the edge.

**Note:** the syllabus uses cm³ and dm³ for volume, not litres or millilitres.

</Step>
