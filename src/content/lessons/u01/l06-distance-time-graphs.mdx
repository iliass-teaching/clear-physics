<Step kind="learn" title="Reading the shape" minutes={12}>

On a distance–time graph, **time** is on the horizontal axis and **distance** is on the vertical axis.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Shape of the line | What the object does |
|---|---|
| flat (horizontal) | at rest: the distance does not change |
| straight and sloping | moves at constant speed |
| curve, getting steeper | speeds up (accelerates) |
| curve, getting less steep | slows down (decelerates) |

</div>

<Step kind="learn" title="Speed from the gradient" minutes={8}>

The **gradient** of the line tells us how steep it is.
On a distance–time graph, the gradient of a straight line **is the speed**:

$$
\text{speed} = \text{gradient} = \frac{\text{change in distance}}{\text{change in time}}
$$

<WorkedExample
  question="Look at part C of the starter graph. Calculate the speed."
  given={['part C starts at $(40\\,\\text{s},\\ 100\\,\\text{m})$', 'part C ends at $(60\\,\\text{s},\\ 250\\,\\text{m})$']}
  find="the speed in part C"
  answer="$7.5\,\text{m/s}$"
>

$\text{change in distance} = 250 - 100 = 150\,\text{m}$

$\text{change in time} = 60 - 40 = 20\,\text{s}$

$\text{speed} = \dfrac{150}{20} = 7.5\,\text{m/s}$

</WorkedExample>

Use two points that are **far apart** on the line, and that are easy to read on the grid.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 20 min, plotting 15 min, practice 15 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- Reading the graph as a picture of the path (a slope = a hill).
- Thinking a flat line means "moving at constant speed".
- Finding the gradient of a later part using the origin, not the start of that part.
- Using too small a scale, or uneven scales.

**Answers for the plotting task:** 0–6 s: 3 m/s; 6–10 s: at rest; 10–16 s: 2 m/s.

**Language support:**

- Teach *steep* and *flat* with your hand before the graphs.
- Story frame: *"First, the … moves at constant speed. Then it stops for … s. Then …"*

**Differentiation:**

- Support: give printed axes with scales already drawn.
- Stretch: in the homework story, which part has the steepest line? Explain why.

**Exam conventions:** axis labels as "quantity / unit"; points as crosses; graph uses more than half of the grid. See "Presentation of data" in the syllabus.

</Step>
