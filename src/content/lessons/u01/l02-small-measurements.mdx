<Step kind="learn" title="Measure many, then divide" minutes={15}>

Some things are too small to measure one by one:

- One page is thinner than 1 mm, the smallest line on a ruler.
- One swing of a pendulum is short. Your **reaction time** (about 0.2 s) is big compared to it.

**The method:** measure many of them together. Then divide by how many there are.

### A small thickness

<WorkedExample
  question="A stack of 200 pages is 1.8 cm thick. Calculate the thickness of one page in mm."
  given={['number of pages $= 200$', 'thickness of the stack $= 1.8\\,\\text{cm}$']}
  find="the thickness of one page, in mm"
  answer="$0.09\,\text{mm}$"
>

Change cm to mm: $1.8\,\text{cm} = 18\,\text{mm}$

$\text{thickness of one page} = \dfrac{18}{200} = 0.09\,\text{mm}$

</WorkedExample>

### A short time: the pendulum

One **oscillation** is one full swing: from one side to the other side, and back again.
The **period** is the time for one oscillation.

1. Start the stopwatch when the pendulum passes the middle. Count "zero".
2. Count each time it passes the middle **in the same direction**: 1, 2, 3 …
3. Stop the stopwatch at 20.
4. $\text{period} = \dfrac{\text{time for 20 oscillations}}{20}$

**Why 20?** Your reaction time adds an error of about 0.2 s. For 20 oscillations, the same error is shared by 20. So the error for one oscillation is 20 times smaller.

### Repeat and find the mean

Do the measurement 3 times. Then find the **mean**: add the readings and divide by 3.

<WorkedExample
  question="A student times 20 oscillations three times. Calculate the mean period."
  given={['times for 20 oscillations: $28.2\\,\\text{s},\\ 28.6\\,\\text{s},\\ 28.4\\,\\text{s}$']}
  find="the mean period"
  answer="$1.42\,\text{s}$"
>

$\text{mean time} = \dfrac{28.2 + 28.6 + 28.4}{3} = 28.4\,\text{s}$

$\text{period} = \dfrac{28.4}{20} = 1.42\,\text{s}$

</WorkedExample>

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="The period of a pendulum" minutes={20}>

**Equipment:** clamp stand, string (about 50 cm), a small mass (the bob), stopwatch.

1. Hang the pendulum from the clamp stand. Put the stand near the edge of the table, with a heavy weight on its base.
2. Pull the bob a small distance to the side (about 10 cm). Let it go.
3. Time **1** oscillation. Do it 3 times.
4. Time **20** oscillations. Do it 3 times.
5. Find the period from each method.

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 15 min, practical 20 min, practice 15 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- Counting "1" when starting the stopwatch, so the count is one too high.
- Counting each pass of the middle in both directions (this counts half oscillations).
- Forgetting to divide by the number of oscillations after finding the mean.
- Mixing cm and mm in the thickness calculation.

**Language support:**

- Show one oscillation with your arm while you say the word.
- Write "measure many → divide" on the board as the key idea.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: use 10 oscillations and numbers that divide easily.
- Stretch: does the period change if you use a heavier bob, or a longer string? Make a prediction, then test it.

**Safety:** put a heavy weight on the base of the clamp stand so it cannot fall.

</Step>
