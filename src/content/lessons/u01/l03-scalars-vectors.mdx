<Step kind="learn" title="Scalars and vectors" minutes={10}>

A **scalar** has a size (a **magnitude**) only.
A **vector** has a magnitude **and a direction**.

<div class="table-wrap">

| Scalars | Vectors |
|---|---|
| distance | force |
| speed | weight |
| time | velocity |
| mass | acceleration |
| energy | momentum |
| temperature | electric field strength |
| | gravitational field strength |

</div>

We draw a vector as an **arrow**:

- the length of the arrow shows the magnitude;
- the arrow points in the direction.

Be careful with pairs: **speed** is a scalar but **velocity** is a vector. **Mass** is a scalar but **weight** is a vector.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Adding two vectors at right angles" minutes={10}>

The **resultant** is one vector that has the same effect as the vectors together.

When two vectors are at right angles, the resultant is the diagonal of a rectangle.

<Figure svg={vectorsSvg(30, 40, '30 N', '40 N', 'R')} caption="Two forces at right angles. R is the resultant." />

### Method 1: calculation

Use Pythagoras for the magnitude, and tan for the direction.

<WorkedExample
  question="Two forces act on a box at right angles: 30 N to the right and 40 N up."
  given={['force 1 $= 30\\,\\text{N}$ to the right', 'force 2 $= 40\\,\\text{N}$ up']}
  find="the magnitude and the direction of the resultant"
  answer="$R = 50\,\text{N}$, at $53^\circ$ to the 30 N force"
>

$R^2 = 30^2 + 40^2 = 900 + 1600 = 2500$

$R = \sqrt{2500} = 50\,\text{N}$

$\tan\theta = \dfrac{40}{30}$, so $\theta = \tan^{-1}(1.33) = 53^\circ$

</WorkedExample>

### Method 2: scale drawing

1. Choose a scale, for example 1 cm = 10 N.
2. Draw the first arrow: 3 cm to the right.
3. From the same point, draw the second arrow: 4 cm up.
4. Complete the rectangle. Draw the diagonal from the starting point.
5. Measure the diagonal: 5 cm. So $R = 5 \times 10 = 50\,\text{N}$.
6. Measure the angle with a protractor.

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Human vectors" minutes={15}>

Work outside or in a large room, in groups of 3. **Equipment:** tape measure, chalk or cones, compass (optional).

1. Mark a start point.
2. Walk 3 m in a straight line. Mark the point.
3. Turn 90° and walk 4 m. Mark the end point.
4. Measure the straight distance from the start to the end.
5. Calculate it with Pythagoras. Compare.

Then do 2 scale drawings on paper:

- 5 N to the right and 12 N up.
- A plane flies north at 80 m/s. The wind blows east at 60 m/s.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**This lesson is Extended content only** (Supplement 1.1 S4–S7). Core students can do the starter and the human vectors activity, then move on.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 20 min, activity 15 min, practice 15 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- Adding the magnitudes of vectors at right angles.
- Thinking speed and velocity are the same.
- Thinking weight is a scalar (weight is a force, so it is a vector).
- Giving $\tan\theta$ as the answer, not $\theta$.

**Language support:**

- Use the treasure starter to link "vector" with "direction" before the word is written.
- Sentence frame: *"… is a vector because it has a magnitude and a direction."*

**Differentiation:**

- Support: use Pythagorean triples (3-4-5, 6-8-10) so the numbers are whole.
- Stretch: two forces at right angles have a resultant of 25 N. One force is 7 N. Find the other force.

**Note:** trigonometry ($\tan^{-1}$) is in the mathematical requirements for Extended candidates only.

</Step>
