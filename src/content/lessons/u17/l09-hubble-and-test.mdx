<Step kind="learn" title="Speed, distance and the Hubble constant (Extended)" minutes={12}>

For a far galaxy, astronomers measure two things:

- its **speed** $v$, from the **change in wavelength** of its light (the redshift): a bigger redshift means a higher speed;
- its **distance** $d$, from the **brightness of a supernova** in that galaxy.

The further the galaxy, the faster it moves away. The speed divided by the distance is the same for all galaxies: the **Hubble constant** $H_0$.

$$
H_0 = \frac{v}{d}
$$

The current estimate is $H_0 = 2.2 \times 10^{-18}\ \text{per second}$ ($\text{s}^{-1}$).

<WorkedExample
  question="Use the best-fit line on the graph to find the Hubble constant."
  given={['the line goes through $(0,\\ 0)$ and $(5 \\times 10^{24}\\,\\text{m},\\ 11 \\times 10^{6}\\,\\text{m/s})$']}
  find="the Hubble constant $H_0$"
  answer="$H_0 = 2.2 \times 10^{-18}\,\text{s}^{-1}$"
>

$H_0 = \dfrac{v}{d} = \dfrac{11 \times 10^{6}}{5 \times 10^{24}}$

$H_0 = 2.2 \times 10^{-18}\,\text{s}^{-1}$

</WorkedExample>

<Step kind="learn" title="The age of the Universe (Extended)" minutes={8}>

A galaxy at distance $d$ moves away at speed $v$. If its speed was always the same, the time since it was at our position is:

$$
t = \frac{d}{v} = \frac{1}{H_0}
$$

This is an **estimate of the age of the Universe**:

$$
\frac{1}{H_0} = \frac{1}{2.2 \times 10^{-18}} = 4.5 \times 10^{17}\,\text{s}
$$

In years ($1\ \text{year} = 3.15 \times 10^{7}\,\text{s}$): $\dfrac{4.5 \times 10^{17}}{3.15 \times 10^{7}} = 1.4 \times 10^{10}$ years, about **14 billion years**.

This is evidence that all the matter in the Universe was at a single point at the start.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** the Hubble constant and the age of the Universe (6.2.3 S7–S11) are Extended. Core students can do the starter, then start the unit test after a short recap of the unit.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 20 min, practice 10 min, unit test 25 min.

**Graph data:** the points are example data, made to fit $H_0 \approx 2.2 \times 10^{-18}\,\text{s}^{-1}$. They are not real measurements.

**Common mistakes:**

- Writing the unit of $H_0$ as s instead of $\text{s}^{-1}$ (per second).
- Giving the age in seconds when the question asks for years.
- Thinking $1/H_0$ is exact. It is an estimate: it assumes the speed of the galaxies has always been the same.

**Using the test:** questions 1–5, 7 and 9 test Core content; 6, 8 and 10 are Extended. The feedback for each wrong answer names the mistake.

**Language support:**

- Read $H_0$ as "H nought" and $\text{s}^{-1}$ as "per second".

**Differentiation:**

- Support: give the equation sheet for Unit 17 during the test the first time.
- Stretch: the accepted age of the Universe is about 13.8 billion years. Why is our estimate a little different?

</Step>
