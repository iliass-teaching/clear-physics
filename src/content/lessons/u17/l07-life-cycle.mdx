<Step kind="starter" title="Stars do not live forever" minutes={5}>

A star uses hydrogen as fuel.

What do you think happens when a star runs out of fuel? Write one idea. Then compare with your partner.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Birth of a star" minutes={10}>

1. A star forms from a **cloud of gas and dust** in space (a **nebula**). The cloud contains hydrogen.
2. Gravity pulls the cloud together. It collapses and gets hotter. This is a **protostar**.
3. The centre becomes very hot. Nuclear fusion starts: hydrogen fuses into helium. The star is now a **stable star**.

**Why is a stable star stable?** Two forces balance:

- **inwards:** the gravitational attraction of the star’s own material;
- **outwards:** a force caused by the very high temperature in the centre of the star.

When these two forces are equal, the star does not get bigger or smaller.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="The end of a star" minutes={10}>

All stars run out of hydrogen in the end. Then:

- **Most stars** expand and become **red giants**.
- **Very massive stars** expand and become **red supergiants**.

<Figure svg={starLifeSvg()} caption="The life cycle of a star." width="38rem" />

**A less massive star** (like the Sun): the red giant forms a **planetary nebula** (a shell of gas), with a **white dwarf** star at its centre.

**A very massive star:** the red supergiant explodes as a **supernova**. This makes a nebula that contains hydrogen and **new, heavier elements**. A **neutron star** or a **black hole** is left at the centre.

The nebula from a supernova can form **new stars, with planets**. The elements in your body, like the carbon and the oxygen, were made in stars.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**This lesson is Extended content** (6.2.2 S3). Core students can use it as enrichment: the ideas help them understand Lesson 4 (where the elements come from).

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 20 min, cards 15 min, check 15 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Exit ticket answer:** cloud of gas and dust → protostar → stable star → red supergiant → supernova → black hole.

**Common mistakes:**

- "All stars explode." Only very massive stars become supernovae.
- "A planetary nebula has planets in it." The name is historical: it is a shell of gas.
- Thinking the star is "on fire".

**Language support:**

- The two branches use similar words: *giant / supergiant*, *white dwarf / neutron star*. Put them side by side on the board.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: give the stable star card already in place.
- Stretch: why does a massive star have a shorter life than the Sun, even though it has more fuel? (It uses its fuel much faster.)

</Step>
