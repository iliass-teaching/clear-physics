<Step kind="learn" title="The Sun is a star" minutes={10}>

The Sun is a **star of medium size**. It is made mostly of **hydrogen** and **helium**.

The Sun gives out (radiates) most of its energy as:

- **infrared** radiation,
- **visible light**,
- **ultraviolet** radiation.

**Extended:** stars are powered by **nuclear reactions** that release energy. In a stable star like the Sun, the reaction is the **nuclear fusion** of hydrogen into helium.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Galaxies and light-years" minutes={12}>

A **galaxy** is a huge group of many billions of stars.
The Sun is one star in our galaxy, the **Milky Way**.

The other stars of the Milky Way are **much further away** from us than the Sun. Distances in space are so big that we use a special unit: the **light-year**.

One **light-year** is the distance that light travels in space in one year. (It is a distance, not a time.)

**Extended:**

$$
1\ \text{light-year} = 9.5 \times 10^{15}\,\text{m}
$$

We can check this value: $\text{distance} = \text{speed} \times \text{time} = 3.0 \times 10^{8}\,\text{m/s} \times 3.15 \times 10^{7}\,\text{s} = 9.5 \times 10^{15}\,\text{m}$.

<WorkedExample
  question="Proxima Centauri is the closest star to the Sun. It is 4.2 light-years away. Calculate this distance in metres."
  given={['distance $= 4.2$ light-years', '$1\\ \\text{light-year} = 9.5 \\times 10^{15}\\,\\text{m}$']}
  find="the distance in metres"
  answer="$4.0 \times 10^{16}\,\text{m}$"
>

$d = 4.2 \times 9.5 \times 10^{15}$

$d = 4.0 \times 10^{16}\,\text{m}$

</WorkedExample>

The light from Proxima Centauri left the star 4.2 years ago.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** 6.2.1 C1 and 6.2.2 C1 are Core. Nuclear fusion in stars (6.2.1 S2) and the value of the light-year (6.2.2 S2) are Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 22 min, distance task 13 min, practice 15 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- "A light-year is a time."
- "The Sun is burning." It is not a chemical fire: nuclear fusion releases its energy.
- "The stars in the sky are in other galaxies." All the stars we see with the naked eye are in the Milky Way.

**Distance table:** values from $t = d/c$: Earth–Moon $3.84 \times 10^{8}\,\text{m}$; Sun–Earth $1.5 \times 10^{11}\,\text{m}$; Sun–Neptune $4.5 \times 10^{12}\,\text{m}$. Proxima Centauri 4.2 light-years; Andromeda 2.5 million light-years.

**Language support:**

- Underline the word *year* in *light-year* and ask: "Is it a time or a distance?"

**Differentiation:**

- Support: Core students do questions 1 and 2 of the task, and Level 1 of the practice.
- Stretch: how many times further away is Proxima Centauri than the Sun? (About $2.7 \times 10^{5}$ times.)

</Step>
