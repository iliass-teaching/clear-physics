Listen to the sound of an ambulance (your teacher plays a video).

1. How does the sound change when the ambulance comes towards you?
2. How does it change when it moves away?

The same kind of change happens to light from objects that move away from us.

<Step kind="learn" title="Billions of galaxies" minutes={5}>

The **Milky Way** is one of **many billions of galaxies** in the **Universe**.
The Milky Way is about **100 000 light-years** across.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Redshift" minutes={12}>

**Redshift** is an increase in the observed **wavelength** of electromagnetic radiation from stars and galaxies that **move away** from us.

When we split the light from a star or a galaxy, we see a spectrum with dark lines. Each element makes lines at fixed wavelengths.

<Figure svg={redshiftSvg()} caption="The same dark lines, shifted towards the red end (longer wavelength) for a distant galaxy." width="36rem" />

For **distant galaxies**, the lines are shifted towards the **red** end of the spectrum, compared with light from a source on the Earth. The light is **redshifted**.

**What does this tell us?**

1. The light from distant galaxies is redshifted, so they are **moving away** from us.
2. Galaxies in every direction are moving away from each other. So the **Universe is expanding**.
3. If the Universe is expanding, then in the past everything was closer together. Long ago, all the matter was at a single point.

This supports the **Big Bang theory**: the Universe began from a single point, and has been expanding since then.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="The cosmic microwave background (Extended)" minutes={8}>

Astronomers detect **microwave radiation** of a specific frequency coming from **all directions** in space.
This is the **cosmic microwave background radiation (CMBR)**.

- The CMBR was produced **shortly after the Universe formed**.
- As the Universe expanded, the radiation was stretched to longer wavelengths. Today it is in the **microwave** region of the electromagnetic spectrum.

The CMBR is more evidence for the Big Bang theory.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** 6.2.3 C1–C4 are Core. The CMBR (S5, S6) is Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 25 min, balloon 15 min, check 10 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- "Redshift means the galaxy is red." It is a shift in wavelength.
- "The Big Bang was an explosion at one place in space." Space itself expands: there is no centre (the balloon surface has no centre either).
- Mixing redshift and the CMBR.

**About the starter:** the change in the sound is the Doppler effect. It is not in the syllabus, but it is a good everyday example of a change in wavelength due to motion.

**Language support:**

- Reading frame for the figure: *"The lines from the galaxy are … than the lines from the Earth. So the wavelength is …"*

**Differentiation:**

- Support: focus on the figure and the three-step reasoning.
- Stretch: in the balloon model, show that the far galaxies move away faster. This is the idea of the next lesson (the Hubble constant).

**Safety:** students with a latex allergy should not handle the balloons.

</Step>
