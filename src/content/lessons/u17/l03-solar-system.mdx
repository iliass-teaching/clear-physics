<Step kind="learn" title="What is in the Solar System?" minutes={10}>

The Solar System contains:

1. **one star**: the Sun;
2. **eight planets** that orbit the Sun;
3. **minor planets** that orbit the Sun: **dwarf planets** like Pluto, and **asteroids** (many are in the asteroid belt, between Mars and Jupiter);
4. **moons** that orbit the planets;
5. smaller objects, like **comets**.

<Figure svg={solarSystemSvg()} caption="The Sun and the eight planets in order. Sizes and distances are not to scale." width="38rem" />

The planets in order from the Sun:

**Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune**

A sentence to help you remember (look at the first letters): *My Very Easy Method Just Speeds Up Naming.*

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="How long does light take?" minutes={8}>

Light is very fast, but the Solar System is very big. Light from the Sun needs time to reach the planets.

$$
\text{time} = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}}
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
t = \frac{d}{c}
$$

The speed of light is $c = 3.0 \times 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$.

<WorkedExample
  question="The Sun is 1.5 × 10¹¹ m from the Earth. How long does sunlight take to reach us?"
  given={['$d = 1.5 \\times 10^{11}\\,\\text{m}$', '$c = 3.0 \\times 10^{8}\\,\\text{m/s}$']}
  find="the time $t$"
  answer="$t = 500\,\text{s}$ (about 8 minutes)"
>

$t = \dfrac{d}{c} = \dfrac{1.5 \times 10^{11}}{3.0 \times 10^{8}}$

$t = 500\,\text{s}$

In minutes: $\dfrac{500}{60} = 8.3\,\text{min}$

</WorkedExample>

So when you see the Sun, you see light that left it about 8 minutes ago. (Never look directly at the Sun.)

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Data about the planets (Extended)" minutes={7}>

<PlanetTable columns={['distance', 'period', 'density', 'temperature', 'g']} />

Look for **patterns**:

- The further the planet, the longer its orbital period.
- The four inner planets have a high density (rock and metal). The four outer planets have a low density (mostly gas).
- In general, the temperature is lower further from the Sun. **But** Venus is hotter than Mercury: its thick atmosphere traps the heat.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** the members of the Solar System (6.1.2 C1) and light travel time (6.1.2 C4) are Core. Analysing planetary data (6.1.2 S8) is Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 25 min, distance model 10 min, practice 15 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- Thinking Pluto is a planet (it is a dwarf planet).
- Forgetting to change km to m before using $c = 3.0 \times 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$.
- Thinking temperature always decreases with distance (Venus is the exception).

**Model distances:** distance from the Sun in 10⁶ km ÷ 100 gives metres in the model (NASA data).

**Language support:**

- Sentence frame for patterns: *"The further the planet is from the Sun, the … its …"*

**Differentiation:**

- Support: give the planet order on a card during the quiz.
- Stretch: Neptune is $4.5 \times 10^{12}\,\text{m}$ from the Sun. How long does sunlight take to reach it, in hours? (About 4.2 h.)

</Step>
