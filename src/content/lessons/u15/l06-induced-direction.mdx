<Step kind="starter" title="Hard to push" minutes={5}>

When you push a strong magnet quickly into a coil connected in a complete circuit, you can feel a small force pushing back. Where does this force come from?

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="The induced e.m.f. opposes the change (Extended)" minutes={15}>

**The direction of an induced e.m.f. always opposes the change that causes it.**

- Push a **N pole** into a coil: the induced current makes the end of the coil near the magnet a **N pole**. It **repels** the magnet: it opposes the movement.
- Pull the N pole **out**: the end of the coil becomes a **S pole**. It **attracts** the magnet: again, it opposes the movement.

**Why must it be like this?** Energy is conserved. To make a current, you must do **work** against this force. If the induced current helped the movement, you would get energy for nothing.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Fleming's right-hand rule (Extended)" minutes={12}>

For a **straight wire moving across a field**, use your **right** hand (the right hand is for **generators**):

- th**u**mb: **m**otion of the wire;
- **F**irst finger: **F**ield (N to S);
- se**C**ond finger: induced **C**urrent (conventional).

**Compare:** the **left**-hand rule is for **motors** (current → force); the **right**-hand rule is for **generators** (motion → current).

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Test the rule" minutes={15}>

<InductionSim />

1. Push the N pole in. Note the direction of the needle.
2. Pull it out. Then turn the magnet round (S pole first) and push it in. Compare the three results.
3. Explain each result: which pole is induced at the end of the coil near the magnet?

**With a wire and a U-magnet:** move a wire down between the poles, then up. Use the right-hand rule to predict the direction of the current each time, then check with the meter.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Extended only** (4.5.1 S4, S5). Core students can repeat the investigation of lesson 15.5 and practise the three factors.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, opposing change 15 min, right-hand rule 12 min, investigation 15 min, check 8 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Note:** this rule is often called Lenz's law. The syllabus only asks for the idea that the induced e.m.f. opposes the change.

**Common mistakes:**

- Using the left hand for induction.
- "The induced pole always attracts the magnet."

**Differentiation:**

- Support: a table "movement / pole of the magnet / induced pole at the near end".
- Stretch: drop a strong magnet down a copper pipe. It falls very slowly. Explain why.

</Step>
