<Step kind="learn" title="The motor effect" minutes={12}>

A wire carrying a current, in a magnetic field, feels a **force**. This is the **motor effect**.

<Figure svg={motorEffectSvg()} caption="A force acts on the wire." width="30rem" />

- The force is at **right angles** to the current and to the field.
- **Reverse the current** → the force **reverses**.
- **Reverse the field** (swap the poles) → the force **reverses**.
- Reverse both → the force is in the **same** direction as at the start.
- There is **no force** if the wire is **parallel** to the field.

The force is bigger with a bigger current and a stronger field.

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="The jumping wire" minutes={15}>

**Equipment:** a strong U-shaped magnet, two horizontal metal rails (or a loose bare copper wire), a short copper rod, a low-voltage supply, a switch.

1. Put the copper rod across the rails, between the poles of the magnet.
2. Switch on for a moment. Which way does the rod roll (or jump)?
3. Reverse the current. What happens?
4. Turn the magnet over (reverse the field). What happens?

<Step kind="learn" title="Fleming's left-hand rule (Extended)" minutes={12}>

Hold the **thumb**, **first finger** and **second finger** of your **left** hand at right angles to each other:

- **F**irst finger: **F**ield (from N to S);
- se**C**ond finger: **C**urrent (conventional current, + to −);
- th**u**mb: **m**otion (the direction of the force).

**Beams of charged particles:** a beam of moving charged particles is a current.

- For **positive** particles, the current is in the direction they move.
- For **electrons** (negative), the conventional current is **opposite** to the direction they move. Point your second finger the **other way**.

Try it in the simulation: tick "a beam of electrons".

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** the experiment and the effect of reversing (4.5.4 C1) are Core. Fleming's left-hand rule (S2) and beams of charged particles (S3) are Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 12 min, practical 15 min, Fleming 12 min, check 11 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- Using the right hand (that is for generators, lesson 15.6).
- Using the electron flow as the current.
- Field direction from S to N.

**Language support:**

- Say the rule aloud: "Field, Current, Motion — first, second, thumb."

**Differentiation:**

- Support: do the simulation with the rule card in front of you.
- Stretch: a beam of electrons in a TV tube is bent by magnetic coils. Which way must the field point to bend the beam upwards?

**Safety:** the rod and rails get hot with a big current; switch on only briefly.

</Step>
