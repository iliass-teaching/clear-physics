A current can make a magnetic field (lesson 15.1). Can a magnet make a current? What do you think you would need to do?

<Step kind="learn" title="Inducing an e.m.f." minutes={12}>

An **e.m.f.** is **induced** in a conductor when:

- the conductor **moves across** a magnetic field (it "cuts" the field lines), or
- the magnetic field through the conductor **changes** (for example, a magnet moves into a coil).

If the conductor is part of a complete circuit, the e.m.f. makes a **current** flow. This is **electromagnetic induction**.

<Figure svg={inductionSvg()} caption="Pushing a magnet into a coil induces an e.m.f." width="32rem" />

- **No movement → no e.m.f.** A magnet that stays still inside a coil induces nothing.
- Pushing the magnet **in** and pulling it **out** give e.m.f.s in **opposite** directions.

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Investigate" minutes={20}>

**Real experiment:** connect a coil to a sensitive centre-zero meter (a galvanometer). Move a bar magnet in and out of the coil. You can also move a straight wire up and down between the poles of a U-shaped magnet.

**Virtual experiment:**

<InductionSim />

Find out what changes the size of the deflection of the needle:

1. Push the magnet in slowly, then quickly.
2. Use 5, 10 and 20 turns.
3. Use the stronger magnet.
4. Stop the magnet inside the coil. What does the needle do?
5. Turn the magnet round. Push it in. Compare with step 1.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="What affects the size of the e.m.f.?" minutes={8}>

The induced e.m.f. is **bigger** with:

1. a **faster** movement (or a faster change of the field);
2. a **stronger** magnet (a stronger field);
3. **more turns** on the coil.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core:** 4.5.1 C1–C3. The direction of the induced e.m.f. comes in the next lesson (Extended).

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 12 min, investigation 20 min, factors 8 min, check 10 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**About the simulation:** the needle shows a value proportional to (number of turns) × (strength of the magnet) × (rate of change of the field through the coil). The numbers on the scale are not given: it is a qualitative model.

**Common mistakes:**

- "A magnet inside a coil makes a current" (only while it moves).
- Confusing induction (motion → current) with the motor effect (current → motion).

**Language support:**

- *induce* = cause, without touching.
- *cut field lines* = move across them.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: a results table with columns "what I changed / what the needle did".
- Stretch: why does a bicycle dynamo light glow brighter when you ride faster?

</Step>
