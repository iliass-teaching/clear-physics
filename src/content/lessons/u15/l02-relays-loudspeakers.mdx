<Step kind="starter" title="Build an electromagnet" minutes={10}>

Wrap 20 turns of insulated wire around an iron nail. Connect it to a cell (briefly). How many paper clips can it pick up? Try with 40 turns. Switch off: what happens to the paper clips?

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Electromagnets" minutes={8}>

An **electromagnet** is a solenoid with a **soft-iron core**. The iron makes the field much stronger.

- It is magnetic **only when the current is on**: soft iron is a temporary magnet (Unit 12).
- It is **stronger** with more current, more turns, and an iron core.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="The relay" minutes={12}>

<Figure svg={relaySvg()} caption="A relay: an electromagnetic switch." width="32rem" />

1. A **small current** flows in the coil of the relay.
2. The coil and its iron core become an electromagnet.
3. It pulls the **iron armature**, which closes the **contacts** of a second circuit.
4. A **big current** can now flow in the second circuit.

**Why use a relay?** A small, safe current (for example from a sensor circuit, or a switch in a car) can switch on a big current (for example a car starter motor, a big motor in a factory, or a mains appliance). The two circuits are separate: this is safer.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="The loudspeaker" minutes={12}>

<Figure svg={loudspeakerSvg()} caption="A loudspeaker." width="30rem" />

1. An **alternating current** (from the amplifier) flows in the **coil**.
2. The coil is in the field of a **permanent magnet**, so it feels a **force**.
3. The current changes direction again and again, so the force changes direction: the coil and the **cone** move **in and out**.
4. The vibrating cone makes the air vibrate: **sound** waves.

A bigger current gives a bigger vibration (a louder sound). A higher frequency of current gives a higher pitch.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core:** 4.5.3 C3.

**Timing:** starter 10 min, electromagnets 8 min, relay 12 min, loudspeaker 12 min, check 13 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Demonstration:** connect a loudspeaker (without its amplifier) to a signal generator at low frequency (2–5 Hz) and low power: the cone visibly moves in and out.

**Common mistakes:**

- "The relay coil is connected to the big current." (The circuits are separate.)
- "The magnet in a loudspeaker vibrates."

**Language support:**

- *armature* = the moving iron part.
- *contacts* = two metal parts that touch to complete a circuit.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: label the relay diagram with five given words.
- Stretch: draw a circuit where a thermistor and a relay switch on a fan when it gets hot.

**Safety:** the wire on the nail gets hot; use a cell, not a power supply, and switch on briefly.

</Step>
