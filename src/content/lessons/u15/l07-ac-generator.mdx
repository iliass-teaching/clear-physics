<Step kind="starter" title="Power stations" minutes={5}>

A power station burns gas, or uses wind or falling water, to turn something. What is turning, and how does it make electricity?

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="A simple a.c. generator (Extended)" minutes={14}>

<Figure svg={generatorSvg()} caption="A simple a.c. generator (rotating coil)." width="34rem" />

- A **coil** is turned in a **magnetic field**. Its sides cut the field lines, so an **e.m.f. is induced**.
- Each half turn, each side of the coil moves the other way across the field, so the e.m.f. **changes direction**: this is **alternating current**.
- **Slip rings** (two complete rings) turn with the coil. **Brushes** press on them. Each end of the coil is always connected to the same output wire, so the a.c. reaches the outside circuit.

**Another design:** a **magnet rotates** inside a fixed coil (for example a bicycle dynamo, and the big generators in power stations). Then no slip rings are needed: the coil does not move.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="The e.m.f.–time graph (Extended)" minutes={10}>

- **Peak (or trough):** the coil is **parallel to the field** (horizontal in the simulation). Its sides move **across** the field lines as fast as possible.
- **Zero:** the coil is **at 90° to the field** (vertical). Its sides move **along** the field lines: they do not cut them.
- Peak and trough: the e.m.f. has the same size but opposite directions (each side of the coil moves the other way).
- **Faster rotation:** bigger peaks **and** more cycles each second (higher frequency).

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Extended only** (4.5.2 S1, S2). Core students can revise induction (lesson 15.5) with the simulation of that lesson.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, generator 14 min, simulation 16 min, graph 10 min, check 10 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**About the simulation:** e.m.f. $= E_0 \sin\alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the angle of the coil from the vertical, and $E_0$ is proportional to the speed.

**Common mistakes:**

- "The e.m.f. is biggest when the coil is vertical (in line with the poles)."
- Confusing slip rings with the split-ring commutator.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: a set of 4 coil positions to match to points on the graph.
- Stretch: sketch the graph for a generator turning at the same speed but with twice as many turns.

</Step>
