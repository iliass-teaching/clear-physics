<Step kind="learn" title="A coil that turns" minutes={14}>

<Figure svg={dcMotorSvg()} caption="A simple d.c. motor." width="34rem" />

A coil carrying a current is between the poles of a magnet.

- On one side of the coil, the current goes one way: the force is **up**.
- On the other side, the current goes the other way: the force is **down**.
- These two forces make a **turning effect** (a moment, Unit 3): the coil **turns**.

**The turning effect is bigger if you increase:**

1. the **number of turns** on the coil;
2. the **current**;
3. the **strength of the magnetic field**.

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="Keeping it turning (Extended)" minutes={12}>

After half a turn, the sides of the coil swap places. If the current stayed the same, the forces would push the coil **back**. It would stop.

The **split-ring commutator** solves this:

- It is a ring cut into two halves, fixed to the coil. It turns with the coil.
- Two **brushes** (carbon blocks) press on it and connect it to the battery.
- Every **half turn**, the halves of the ring swap brushes, so the **current in the coil reverses**.
- So the side of the coil near the N pole always has the current in the same direction: the forces always turn the coil the **same way**.

When the coil is vertical, the brushes touch the gaps; the coil keeps turning because it is already moving (its momentum).

</Step>

<Step kind="do" title="Build a simple motor" minutes={17}>

**Equipment:** a motor kit (coil on a wooden block, magnets, a split-ring commutator made of tape and wire, brushes) **or** a "homopolar motor": a battery, a strong button magnet and a shaped copper wire.

1. Build the motor following the kit instructions.
2. Make it turn. Then:
   - reverse the battery: what happens?
   - turn the magnets round: what happens?
   - add a second cell (bigger current): what happens?
3. Explain each result with the motor effect.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** the turning effect and the three factors (4.5.5 C1) are Core. The commutator and brushes (S2) are Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, learn 26 min, build 17 min, check 7 min, exit ticket 5 min.

**Common mistakes:**

- Confusing slip rings (a.c. generator, lesson 15.7) and the split-ring commutator (d.c. motor).
- "The commutator reverses the battery."

**Language support:**

- *commutator* = a switch that changes (commutes) the direction of the current.
- *brush* = a block that rubs (brushes) on the ring.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: a diagram with the forces to add.
- Stretch: why do real motors have many coils at different angles, and curved magnets?

**Safety:** strong (neodymium) magnets can pinch fingers. Homopolar motors: the wire gets hot; do not leave connected.

</Step>
