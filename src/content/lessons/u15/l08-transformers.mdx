<Step kind="learn" title="The transformer" minutes={14}>

<Figure svg={transformerSvg()} caption="A simple transformer." width="30rem" />

A transformer has:

- a **soft-iron core** (often a closed square ring of thin iron sheets);
- a **primary coil** (the input), with $N_p$ turns;
- a **secondary coil** (the output), with $N_s$ turns.

$$
\frac{V_p}{V_s} = \frac{N_p}{N_s}
$$

- **Step-up** transformer: more turns on the secondary ($N_s > N_p$), so $V_s > V_p$.
- **Step-down** transformer: fewer turns on the secondary ($N_s < N_p$), so $V_s < V_p$.

<WorkedExample
  question="A transformer has 2000 turns on the primary coil and 100 turns on the secondary coil. The primary p.d. is 230 V. Calculate the secondary p.d."
  given={['$V_p = 230\\,\\text{V}$', '$N_p = 2000$', '$N_s = 100$']}
  find="the secondary p.d. $V_s$"
  answer="$V_s = 11.5\,\text{V}$ (a step-down transformer)"
>

$\dfrac{V_p}{V_s} = \dfrac{N_p}{N_s}$, so $V_s = V_p \times \dfrac{N_s}{N_p}$

$V_s = 230 \times \dfrac{100}{2000}$

$V_s = 11.5\,\text{V}$

</WorkedExample>

</Step>

<Step kind="learn" title="How it works (Extended)" minutes={12}>

1. An **alternating** current in the primary coil makes an **alternating magnetic field** in the soft-iron core.
2. The core carries this changing field through the **secondary coil**.
3. The changing field **induces** an alternating e.m.f. in the secondary coil (electromagnetic induction).

A transformer works **only with a.c.**: a steady d.c. makes a field that does not change, so nothing is induced.

**Power:** if the transformer is **100% efficient**, power in = power out:

$$
I_p V_p = I_s V_s
$$

So a step-down transformer gives a **bigger** current in the secondary than in the primary.

</Step>

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** construction, terms and the turns ratio (4.5.6 C1–C3) are Core. How it works and $I_p V_p = I_s V_s$ (S6, S7) are Extended.

**Timing:** starter 5 min, transformer 14 min, how it works 12 min, practice 22 min, exit ticket 7 min.

**Demonstration:** a demountable transformer (two coils on a C-core) with a low-voltage a.c. supply and lamps. Show what happens with d.c.

**Common mistakes:**

- Turning the ratio upside down.
- "A step-up transformer gives more energy." (Power out ≤ power in: the current goes down.)
- Using d.c.

**Language support:**

- *primary* = first (input); *secondary* = second (output).
- *step up* = make bigger; *step down* = make smaller.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: level 1 only, with the equation rearranged on the board.
- Stretch: real transformers are about 95–99% efficient. Where does the lost energy go?

**Safety:** use only low-voltage a.c. supplies for demonstrations. Never open a mains transformer.

</Step>
