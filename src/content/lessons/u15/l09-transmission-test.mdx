<Figure svg={transmissionSvg()} caption="Transformers in the electricity grid." width="38rem" />

1. A **step-up** transformer at the power station increases the p.d. to a very high value (hundreds of kilovolts).
2. **Cables** (on pylons) carry the electricity over long distances.
3. **Step-down** transformers near towns reduce the p.d. to a safe value for homes (230 V in many countries; 220 V in Thailand).

**Advantages of high-voltage transmission:**

- For the same power, a high p.d. means a **small current** in the cables.
- A small current means **less energy lost** as heat in the cables.
- So **thinner, cheaper** cables can be used, and less energy is wasted.

**Extended: why less loss?** The power lost as heat in a cable of resistance $R$ is:

$$
P = I^2 R
$$

$P = IV$: for the same power, if the p.d. is 10 times bigger, the current is 10 times smaller, and the power loss is $10^2 = 100$ times smaller.

<Step kind="teacher" title="Teacher notes">

**Core and Extended:** the grid and its advantages (4.5.6 C4, C5) are Core. $P = I^2R$ (S8) is Extended.

**Timing:** transmission 12 min, practice 15 min, unit test 33 min.

**Numbers:** typical values: generators about 25 kV; transmission up to 400 kV in many countries (for example the UK national grid); homes 230 V (220 V in Thailand). These are examples, not syllabus requirements.

**Using the test:** questions 1–8 test Core content; 9–10 are Extended.

**Common mistakes:**

- "High voltage means high current."
- Forgetting to square the current in $P = I^2R$.

**Differentiation:**

- Support: the grid diagram to label.
- Stretch: a power station sends 100 MW. Compare the power lost in a 5 Ω cable at 25 kV and at 400 kV.
